# Playbooks: Relationships and Leadership department

Step-by-step plans for situations owners bring here, built from the 17 Relationships and Leadership books (R01 to R17) and the frameworks in frameworks.md. Quote policy: verbatim quotes appear only from the author talks listed in talks.md, always with a T id; copyrighted book text is never quoted; everything else is paraphrase cited by book id.

## 1. Co-founder or partner conflict
**Diagnostic questions:** What exactly do you disagree about: roles, money, effort, direction or respect? When did you last talk about it directly? What do you really want for the business, for them and for the friendship? Is there a written agreement on roles, decisions and exit?
**Steps:**
1. Prepare with Start with Heart: write what you want for yourself, them and the relationship (Crucial Conversation Prep, R12).
2. Separate facts from your story about their motives (Master Your Story, R12; The Pause, R04).
3. Meet in person. Open with shared purpose, then loop their view until they agree you have it (R12, Looping, R16).
4. Sort which conversation it is: practical (roles, money), emotional (feeling unvalued) or identity (who leads) (Three Conversations, R16).
5. Agree who decides what, and write it down (Disagree and Commit, R05, R08).
6. If reactions to distance and check-ins drive the friction, agree a fixed contact rhythm, used as an analogy only (Attachment Lens, R17).
**Metric or sign:** time between a problem appearing and you discussing it; aim for days, not months.
**Common mistake:** arguing about tasks when the real issue is respect or identity, or venting by text.

## 2. An underperformer I keep avoiding
**Diagnostic questions:** Have you told them plainly, with examples? Do they know what good looks like (outcomes, not duties)? Is it skill, will or a role mismatch? How long has it gone on?
**Steps:**
1. Check yourself on the 2x2: months of silence is ruinous empathy (Radical Candor 2x2, R13).
2. Write two or three outcomes for the role so the gap is clear (Scorecard, R07).
3. Hold the conversation in private within a week: context, what you saw, the result, the next step (Get, Give, Encourage, R13; STATE, R12).
4. Ask what is getting in the way; loop the answer (R16).
5. Set dated next steps and support matched to their task maturity (R08).
6. If the pattern holds after clear feedback and support, act (Firing Done Right, R09).
**Metric or sign:** agreed outcomes met by the dated check-in.
**Common mistake:** firing someone who was never told, or telling them so gently they did not hear it.

## 3. Firing someone, including a friend or family member
**Diagnostic questions:** Have they had clear feedback and a fair chance? Is the decision final? What will you tell the rest of the team? What do you owe them in terms, references and dignity?
**Steps:**
1. Confirm you have held the crucial conversation before (R12, R13).
2. Decide, and do not let it drag or leak; delay is decision debt (Management Debt, R09).
3. Tell them yourself, in person, with clear reasons and fair terms (Firing Done Right, R09; Right Medium, R14).
4. Separate the relationship from the role: say what you value about them as a person (R01, R12).
5. Tell the team the same day, without blaming the person leaving (R09, Circle of Safety, R14).
**Metric or sign:** the team's reaction a week later: relief and steadiness, not fear.
**Common mistake:** keeping a friend in a role for months because of the friendship, which hurts the team and the friendship.

## 4. Making a key hire
**Diagnostic questions:** What outcomes must this person deliver in the first year? Have you ever done the job? Who in your network knows someone good? What will make the role attractive to a strong candidate?
**Steps:**
1. Write a scorecard with mission, outcomes with numbers and dates, and competencies (A Method, R07).
2. If the role is above your experience, talk to people who have done it well before you interview (R09).
3. Source through referrals first (R07, Generosity-First Networking, R10).
4. Run a career-history interview and call references, including blind ones (R07, R09).
5. Check how they treat juniors and peers (Giver, Taker, Matcher Screen, R11; Character by Pattern, R04).
6. Sell on fit, family, freedom, fortune and fun (R07).
**Metric or sign:** scorecard outcomes hit at 90 days and one year.
**Common mistake:** hiring on charm or on similarity to yourself. Hand off the pay budget to Business if cash is the question.

## 5. A leadership team that is polite but nothing changes
**Diagnostic questions:** Does anyone admit mistakes in meetings? When did someone last disagree with you openly? Are decisions reopened? Do managers defend their department over the business?
**Steps:**
1. Find the lowest broken level of the pyramid (Five Dysfunctions, R05).
2. Go first: name a mistake you made (R05, Belonging Cues, R15).
3. Mine for disagreement: ask each person for the risk in the plan (R05).
4. Decide clearly and log decisions (Disagree and Commit, R05, R08).
5. Ask peers to hold each other to commitments in the meeting (R05).
6. Put one shared team result on the agenda each week (R05).
**Metric or sign:** number of decisions reopened per month; aim for zero without new facts.
**Common mistake:** chasing harmony and calling it trust.

## 6. Everyone waits for me to decide
**Diagnostic questions:** Do your managers know the goal and the limits? Who was punished last time they decided wrong? How experienced is each person in the task? (If the issue is your own calendar, hand off to Productivity.)
**Steps:**
1. Write the intent and limits for each area (Decentralized Command, R06).
2. Rate each manager's task maturity and set the check-in rhythm (R08).
3. When they decide wrong inside the limits, review the process, not the person (Extreme Ownership Review, R06).
4. Ask "what would you do?" before answering (Let the Idea Be Theirs, R01).
**Metric or sign:** number of decisions escalated to you per week.
**Common mistake:** delegating the task but keeping the decision.

## 7. Promoting a top performer to manager
**Diagnostic questions:** Do they want to manage, or just want status and pay? Do they want steep growth or steady excellence? Who will train them?
**Steps:**
1. Hold a career conversation first (Career Conversations, R13).
2. If yes, set a scorecard for the manager role, not the old one (R07).
3. Manage them closely at first: they are new to this task (Task-Relevant Maturity, R08).
4. Teach one-on-ones and feedback in the first month (One-on-Ones, R08; R13).
5. Give a safe way back if it does not fit (R13).
**Metric or sign:** their team's output and retention at six months.
**Common mistake:** assuming a great technician will be a great manager without support.

## 8. I never hear bad news until it is too late
**Diagnostic questions:** What happened to the last person who brought you bad news? Do you ask for criticism? Do people see you change after feedback?
**Steps:**
1. Ask a specific question for feedback in your own words and act on the answer (Get, Give, Encourage, R13).
2. Thank the messenger publicly (Belonging Cues, R15).
3. Admit your own mistakes first (R05, R15).
4. Hold regular one-on-ones where reports set the agenda (R08).
5. Make bad news travel fast a stated rule (R09).
**Metric or sign:** how many problems reach you from staff before customers.
**Common mistake:** asking "any feedback?" and taking silence as good news.

## 9. Building a network from scratch
**Diagnostic questions:** What are you trying to build in the next year? Who already knows you (dormant ties)? What can you offer others? How much time per week can you give?
**Steps:**
1. Write the goal and list 20 to 50 people who matter to it, including old contacts (Generosity-First Networking, R10; R11).
2. Before each contact, learn what they care about and plan ways to help (R10).
3. Lead with help: an intro, an idea, a useful note (Otherish Giving, R11; R02).
4. Go deep: ask what they hope for and struggle with (R10, Deep questions, R16).
5. Follow up within days; keep light, regular contact (R10).
6. Cap giving at a set time each week (R11).
**Metric or sign:** warm introductions received per quarter.
**Common mistake:** leading with an ask or a bargain. Selling to prospects is a Business question.

## 10. Office politics or a power player
**Diagnostic questions:** Who gains if you fail? Who feels threatened by you? Is this inside your own team or outside it (a client, board, investor, family faction)?
**Steps:**
1. Map each person's interest, fear and insecurity (Power Map, R03, R04).
2. Check that your plan does not make anyone senior feel outshone (R03).
3. Judge people by patterns, not words (Character by Pattern, R04).
4. Run a defence check for pressure tactics (Persuasion and Power Defence, R02, R03).
5. Inside your own team, fix it with openness, not counter-moves (Five Dysfunctions, R05; Circle of Safety, R14).
**Metric or sign:** decisions made in meetings, not in corridors.
**Common mistake:** fighting politics with politics inside your own small team, which teaches everyone to play.

## 11. Rebuilding trust after layoffs, pay cuts or a broken promise
**Diagnostic questions:** What did staff hear, and from whom? Did leaders share the pain? What are people afraid of now?
**Steps:**
1. Tell the truth plainly, in person, to everyone (Right Medium, R14, R16; R09).
2. Show leaders taking the first hit (Circle of Safety, R14).
3. Own your part in what went wrong (Extreme Ownership Review, R06).
4. Give time: one-on-ones with each person in the next two weeks (R08, R14).
5. Make small promises and keep them visibly (R12, synthesis of R14, R15).
**Metric or sign:** voluntary departures in the next three months.
**Common mistake:** a cheerful all-hands email with no leader sacrifice.

## 12. Keeping culture as the team grows
**Diagnostic questions:** What are your top three priorities, in order? Do new hires feel they belong in week one? Who in the team shows contempt or disengagement?
**Steps:**
1. Name and rank priorities, with short phrases and rules of thumb (R15).
2. Build belonging cues into onboarding: a buddy, a welcome, future plans (Belonging Cues, R15).
3. Hire against values as well as outcomes, and screen for takers (R07, R11).
4. Deal quickly with one person who poisons the group (R15, R09).
5. Keep teams small enough that the leader knows everyone (R14, R06).
**Metric or sign:** first-90-day turnover.
**Common mistake:** a values poster with no stories, no ranking and no consequences.

## 13. Two employees in conflict
**Diagnostic questions:** Is it about the work, about respect, or about identity and status? Have they talked directly? Is one of them a taker or bully?
**Steps:**
1. Meet each alone and loop their view (Looping, R16).
2. Sort the kind of conversation (Three Conversations, R16).
3. Bring them together around a shared purpose (R12).
4. Agree who does what by when, and follow up (R12).
5. If one person shows a pattern of contempt, treat it as a performance issue (R15, R13).
**Metric or sign:** whether they raise the next problem with each other, not with you.
**Common mistake:** splitting the difference without hearing either fully.

## 14. A family member in the business
**Diagnostic questions:** Is the role defined with outcomes? Who manages them, and can that person give honest feedback? Where are family and business conversations being mixed?
**Steps:**
1. Write a scorecard for their role like anyone else's (R07; same checks as any hire, R03).
2. Separate family talks from work talks: different times and places (Three Conversations, R16).
3. Hold hard talks with Start with Heart and mutual respect (R12).
4. Give feedback privately and let them save face (R01, R13).
**Metric or sign:** other staff see the same standards applied.
**Common mistake:** letting family status replace accountability, which erodes trust with other staff.

## 15. A partner, investor or landlord pushing hard
**Diagnostic questions:** What do they really need? What pressure tactics are in play? What is your walk-away point? (Price negotiation with customers goes to Business.)
**Steps:**
1. Pause before agreeing (The Pause, R04).
2. Run a defence check: is the deadline real, the gift a setup, the authority real? (R02, R03).
3. Map their interests and fears (Power Map, R03).
4. Open the real conversation with mutual purpose (R12).
5. Frame your ask around their self-interest (R01, R03).
**Metric or sign:** decisions made on the merits, after a pause, not under pressure.
**Common mistake:** agreeing on the spot to relieve the discomfort.

## 16. My close relationship is straining the business (use with care)
**Diagnostic questions:** Is your partner or spouse involved in the business? What is the conflict about: time, money, roles, or feeling unheard? Is anyone in distress beyond normal strain?
**Steps:**
1. If there is serious distress, anxiety or a relationship crisis, hand off to Psychology and Mental Health first.
2. Hold the conversation in the right kind: listen to the feeling before offering fixes (Three Conversations, R16; Looping, R16).
3. State needs plainly and agree rhythms of contact and time together (Attachment Lens, R17).
4. If they work in the business, define roles and decision rights in writing (R12, R07).
5. Time and calendar fixes go to Productivity.
**Metric or sign:** fewer repeated fights about the same issue.
**Common mistake:** treating a feelings conversation as a planning problem, or labeling a partner with a style.
In Charles Duhigg's words: "the goal of communication is not just to exchange information. The goal of communication is to feel connected to each other." (T134)
