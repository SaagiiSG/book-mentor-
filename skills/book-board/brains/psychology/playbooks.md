# Playbooks: Psychology and Mental Health department

Built from the 15 Psychology and Mental Health books (H01 to H15) and the frameworks in frameworks.md. Each playbook is a situation owners bring, with questions to ask first, a plan, the sign to watch, and the common mistake. Quote policy: verbatim quotes appear only from the author talks in talks.md, always with a T id; copyrighted book text is never quoted; everything else is paraphrase with book ids.

**Safety first in every playbook.** Before using any plan below, check the When to Get Help Ladder (framework 34). If the person mentions thoughts of suicide or self-harm, harming others, abuse, a medical emergency, or severe symptoms (not sleeping for days, unable to function, psychosis-like experiences, heavy substance withdrawal), stop the book advice, respond with care and point to immediate help: the local emergency number, a crisis line (988 in the US and Canada, 116 123 Samaritans in the UK and Ireland, or findahelpline.com elsewhere) and a trusted person nearby. If symptoms are persistent or worsening, recommend a licensed professional alongside the plan. Never diagnose, never use a diagnosis label for the user, and never advise on medication or substances.

## 1. I'm burned out and cannot stop

**Ask first**
- What are you most tired of: the work, the people, or the feeling that nothing is ever enough?
- When did you last have a full day off with no work at all?
- How are you sleeping, and how is your mood on days off?
- Who in your life knows how tired you are?

**Plan**
1. Separate the stressor list from the stress in your body (Complete the Stress Cycle, H08).
2. Add one cycle completer every day this week, chosen from movement, breath, connection, laughter or making something (H08).
3. Set a sleep window and keep it for two weeks (Sleep Opportunity Floor, H05).
4. Check the three parts: exhaustion, cynicism toward customers or staff, and a sense that nothing you do matters (H08). Note which is worst.
5. Tell two people and set up a Mutual Care Circle (H08, H12).
6. Hand the workload redesign to Productivity, and staffing to Business.

**Sign to watch:** mood on days off. If it stays low most days for two weeks or more, or you cannot enjoy anything, see a doctor or therapist; burnout and depression can overlap.
**Common mistake:** waiting until the business calms down to recover. The cycle can be completed now (H08).

## 2. I'm anxious about the business all the time

**Ask first**
- What is the specific fear, in one sentence?
- Is it a problem you can act on this week, or a worry about something you cannot control?
- What does the anxiety do in your body, and when is it worst?
- How much are you sleeping and how much caffeine are you drinking?

**Plan**
1. Write the fear and run the Ten Distortions Check (H03). Mark fortune-telling and catastrophizing.
2. If it is checkable, do a Thought Record and gather evidence (H03).
3. If it repeats no matter what, use Defusion (H07) and make room for the feeling (Expansion, H07).
4. Split actionable from not: plan one action for the actionable part (planful problem-solving, H08).
5. Daily cycle completer and a caffeine cutoff (H08, H05).
6. Choose one value to act on while still anxious (Values Compass, H07).

**Sign to watch:** whether worry stops you doing key tasks. Anxiety most days for two weeks or more, panic attacks, or avoidance of daily life means see a professional.
**Common mistake:** waiting to feel calm before acting. Some anxiety comes with meaningful work (H07).

## 3. I can't sleep, or I'm running on five hours

**Ask first**
- Are you not giving yourself the chance to sleep, or are you in bed and unable to sleep (T115 makes this split)?
- What time do you wake on weekdays and weekends?
- Caffeine and alcohol: how much and when?
- Is your mind racing at night, and about what?

**Plan**
1. Fix a wake time and an eight-hour window (Sleep Opportunity Floor, H05).
2. Run the Caffeine and Alcohol Audit for a week (H05).
3. Wind down 30 to 60 minutes; put a notebook by the bed for worries (H05, H03).
4. Fit hard work to your chronotype (Chronotype Fit, H05).
5. If lying awake, get up and do something quiet, then return (H05).

**Sign to watch:** sleep for two weeks. If insomnia lasts for weeks, or you have loud snoring with gasping or dangerous daytime sleepiness, see a doctor; CBT for insomnia is a strong first treatment (H05). Not sleeping for days is a reason for urgent help.
**Common mistake:** treating sleep as the first thing to cut in a busy week. Also, quoting the book's scariest numbers as fact; several are disputed.

## 4. I'm on my phone constantly

**Ask first**
- What do you reach for first: email, sales dashboard, social media, news?
- What feeling comes just before you reach?
- How many times a day, roughly?
- What would you do with the time if the phone were gone?

**Plan**
1. Four C's Reflection on the main app (H04).
2. Self-Binding: remove the worst app from the phone, set check times, charge the phone outside the bedroom (H04).
3. Run a 30-day break on one app (H04).
4. Expect the first one to two weeks to feel worse (H04).
5. Agree household or team norms (Phone Boundaries, H13).
6. Notice the feeling before the reach and use a cycle completer instead (H08).

**Sign to watch:** checks per day and morning anxiety after two and four weeks.
**Common mistake:** relying on willpower with the phone in your hand. Distance beats discipline (H04).

## 5. I'm drinking more to wind down

**Ask first**
- How much, how often, and has it increased?
- Do you feel shaky, sweaty or unwell if you skip it?
- Has anyone close to you raised it?
- What does the drink do for you that nothing else does right now?

**Plan**
1. If you drink heavily every day or feel unwell without it, talk to a doctor before cutting down; stopping suddenly can be dangerous. This department does not advise on changing substance use.
2. Four C's Reflection (H04), done honestly.
3. Find other cycle completers for the end of the day (H08).
4. Check sleep effects (Caffeine and Alcohol Audit, H05).
5. Tell one trusted person (radical honesty, H04; shame resilience, H09).

**Sign to watch:** whether use keeps rising or you cannot keep your own limits. If so, see a doctor or addiction professional; Lembke says repeated failure to quit alone means a higher level of care (T114).
**Common mistake:** hiding it out of shame, or quitting cold turkey alone after heavy daily use.

## 6. I feel flat, nothing feels good anymore

**Ask first**
- How long has it been, and is it most days?
- Can you still enjoy anything, even small things?
- Sleep, appetite and energy: any big changes?
- Any thoughts that life is not worth living? (If yes, follow Safety now.)

**Plan**
1. If most days for two weeks or more, recommend a doctor or licensed therapist first, alongside what follows.
2. Small actions before motivation: one planned, doable activity a day and rate how it went versus how you predicted (H03).
3. Connection Audit and one recurring contact (H12, H08).
4. Sleep window (H05).
5. Junk values check if success feels empty (H12, H07).
6. Self-Compassion Break when self-criticism shows up (H14).

**Sign to watch:** whether mood lifts at all over two to four weeks. Worsening means more help, sooner.
**Common mistake:** calling it laziness or treating it as a productivity problem. Hand productivity tactics off only once mood is addressed.

## 7. I keep second-guessing, or I'm too sure about a big call

**Ask first**
- What is the decision and the deadline?
- How sure are you, and what is that confidence based on?
- Have you done something like this before, with fast feedback?
- Are you deciding tired, angry or excited?

**Plan**
1. When to Trust Your Gut test (H01).
2. Outside View for timelines and budgets (H01).
3. Premortem with one or two others (H01).
4. Substitution Check and Loss Frame Check (H01).
5. Decide rested, not at night (H05).
6. Hand the business case itself to Business.

**Sign to watch:** whether the premortem changed the plan. If it changed nothing, you may not have been honest.
**Common mistake:** believing that knowing about biases protects you. Process does; awareness alone does not (H01).

## 8. I failed publicly and I can't stop beating myself up

**Ask first**
- What happened, and what do you tell yourself it means about you?
- Is this about what you did or who you are?
- Who have you talked to about it?
- What would you say to a friend in the same spot?

**Plan**
1. Shame Resilience: name it, check the message, tell one trusted person (H09).
2. Self-Compassion Break daily for a week (H14).
3. Thought Record on the worst thought (H03).
4. Separate the behavior to repair from your worth; plan the repair (H09, H14 fierce side).
5. Arena short list: whose opinion counts now (H09).

**Sign to watch:** whether self-talk shifts from labels (I am an idiot) to behavior (I made a bad call).
**Common mistake:** thinking harshness will stop it happening again. The books say it tends to drive hiding and avoidance (H14, H09).

## 9. I'm afraid to put myself out there

**Ask first**
- What exactly would you be putting out (a post, a pitch, a price, a talk)?
- Whose reaction are you most afraid of?
- What is the worst you imagine, and how likely is it?
- What value would showing up serve?

**Plan**
1. Arena and Your Short List (H09).
2. Separation of Tasks: your task is the work; their reaction is theirs (H10).
3. Story Rewrite and Small Tests: start with a small audience (H15).
4. Defusion on the fear story (H07).
5. Act with the fear present (Expansion, H07).

**Sign to watch:** number of small exposures done per week.
**Common mistake:** waiting to feel ready. Marketing strategy itself hands off to Business.

## 10. I'm an introvert and leading or selling drains me

**Ask first**
- What drains you most: crowds, back-to-back calls, open office, conflict?
- Is it a preference for quiet, or fear of being judged?
- Where do you recharge?
- What are you best at in quiet?

**Plan**
1. Shy or Introvert Check (H11).
2. Stimulation Budget with recovery blocks (H11).
3. Restorative niches at work: a closed door, a walk (H11).
4. Treat events as a few one-on-one conversations (H11, T122).
5. Play to strengths: written updates, prepared meetings, one-on-ones (H11).

**Sign to watch:** energy at the end of high-stimulation days.
**Common mistake:** using the introvert label to avoid all sales or leadership. Free trait theory says you can stretch for what you care about, then recover (H11).

## 11. It's lonely at the top

**Ask first**
- Who can you be fully honest with about the business?
- When did you last spend time with friends with no work talk?
- Do you have peers who face similar decisions?
- Have you moved, exited, or cut people off recently?

**Plan**
1. Connection Audit (H12).
2. Join or start a peer group (Mutual Care Circle, H08).
3. One recurring non-work commitment (H12).
4. Share one real struggle with one person (H09).
5. Check whether status markers have replaced relationships (Junk Values Check, H12).

**Sign to watch:** number of honest conversations per week.
**Common mistake:** thinking the next milestone will fix it.

## 12. I keep snapping at my team

**Ask first**
- When does it happen: time of day, type of issue, how tired you are?
- What do you feel in your body just before?
- How is your sleep and stress load?
- What do you want people to feel after talking to you?

**Plan**
1. Pause the Hijack: learn the early signs and take a timed break (H06).
2. Body First, Then Words (H02, H08).
3. Sleep window, because sleep loss makes reactions stronger (H05).
4. Five Steps to Grow One EI Skill, with honest feedback (H06).
5. Then and Now check if reactions feel bigger than the situation (H02).
6. Repair conversations and team communication hand off to Relationships and Leadership.

**Sign to watch:** outbursts per week, and whether staff bring problems to you early.
**Common mistake:** apologizing without changing the conditions (sleep, load) that feed the hijack.

## 13. Old stuff keeps getting triggered

**Ask first**
- Do certain people, tones or situations set off reactions much bigger than the moment?
- Do you have flashbacks, nightmares, or feel numb or on guard a lot?
- Is this getting worse?
- Do you have support right now?

**Plan**
1. Recommend a trauma-trained licensed therapist. This department gives awareness, not trauma treatment.
2. Body First, Then Words for moments of flooding (H02).
3. Then and Now grounding (H02).
4. Lean on safe people; support after hard events matters a lot (H02, H12).
5. Do not use H10's view that the past does not cause the present here.

**Sign to watch:** frequency and size of reactions, and whether daily function is affected.
**Common mistake:** trying to process trauma alone from a book, or forcing yourself to retell it without support.

## 14. I can't say no, and I need everyone to like me

**Ask first**
- Who is hardest to say no to?
- What do you fear will happen if they are disappointed?
- Whose task is their disappointment?
- What did saying yes cost you last month?

**Plan**
1. Separation of Tasks on three recent requests (H10).
2. Set one boundary framed as what you will do (H15, T126).
3. Fierce Self-Compassion: protection as care (H14).
4. Story Rewrite on the fear of not being liked (H15).
5. The words to use with clients and staff hand off to Relationships and Leadership.

**Sign to watch:** number of clear noes per week, and whether you held the boundary each time.
**Common mistake:** setting a boundary once and dropping it; inconsistency teaches people to push (T126 context).

## 15. Should I see a therapist?

**Ask first**
- What would you want help with?
- How long has it been going on and is it getting worse?
- Has it affected work, sleep or relationships?
- What has stopped you so far?

**Plan**
1. Use the When to Get Help Ladder (framework 34).
2. Treat it like hiring an expert: look for a licensed professional, ask about their approach and experience with your issue, and expect a few sessions to judge fit (H15).
3. Name the presenting problem, and stay open to the underlying one (H15).
4. Use self-help tools between sessions if the therapist agrees (H03, H07, H14).

**Sign to watch:** after four to six sessions, do you feel understood and is something shifting?
**Common mistake:** waiting until crisis, or quitting after one session with a poor fit instead of trying another.

## 16. I can't switch off, even on holiday

**Ask first**
- What happens in your body when you do not check?
- What do you fear will happen if you are unreachable?
- Has anyone covered for you before?
- What did you enjoy before the business?

**Plan**
1. Four C's Reflection on work itself (H04).
2. Self-Binding: an out-of-office, a delegated contact, email off the phone (H04).
3. Expansion for the anxiety of not checking (H07).
4. Cycle completers that are not work (H08).
5. Values Compass: what else you stand for (H07).
6. Coverage and delegation systems hand off to Productivity.

**Sign to watch:** whether you can go a full day without checking and feel okay by day three.
**Common mistake:** a holiday with the laptop open, which keeps the stress cycle running.

## 17. A team member is struggling with their mental health

**Ask first**
- What have you noticed (changes in work, mood, attendance)?
- Has the person said anything about their wellbeing?
- Any sign of risk to their safety? (If yes, follow Safety now and help them reach help.)
- What support does your business offer (time off, an employee assistance program, health cover)?

**Plan**
1. Talk privately, with care, about what you have seen; ask how they are (Three Kinds of Empathy, H06).
2. Listen; do not diagnose or play therapist.
3. Point to professional help and any support you offer.
4. Agree practical adjustments for a set period, and check in.
5. Look at work conditions you control: control, recognition, load (H12, H08).
6. Legal duties and HR process vary by country; check local rules. Management conversations hand off to Relationships and Leadership.

**Sign to watch:** whether they feel safe telling you how they are.
**Common mistake:** either ignoring it or trying to fix it yourself. Your role is care and practical support, not treatment.
