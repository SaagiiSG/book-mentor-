# Playbooks: Business Growth department

Step-by-step plans for common owner situations, built from the 25 Business Growth books (G01 to G06, G08 to G14, G16 to G19, G23 to G30) and the frameworks in frameworks.md. All paraphrased; verbatim quotes only from talks.md sources, with a T id.

## 1. "Revenue has plateaued"
**Ask first:**
- Is the limit demand (not enough buyers) or capacity (cannot deliver more)?
- Which step from lead to delivered job is slowest or most backed up?
- Has the margin per job held, or are you selling more for less?
- Which customers and offers make the most profit?
**Plan:**
1. Get the real numbers by offer and customer, not the story [G01].
2. Map the flow from lead to cash and find the constraint with the Five Focusing Steps [G23].
3. If the constraint is delivery, exploit and subordinate before adding cost [G23, G17].
4. If the constraint is demand, hand off to Marketing or Sales with the diagnosis.
5. Check focus with the Hedgehog Concept; cut low-profit lines [G01].
6. Set one 90-day Rock aimed at the constraint and track it weekly [G03].
**Metric to watch:** throughput: profitable jobs completed per month.
**Common mistake:** spending on more leads when the business cannot deliver more work.

## 2. "I can't find time to work on the business"
**Ask first:**
- What did you do last week, hour by hour?
- What breaks when you are away for a week?
- Which one function drives the most success here?
- Who could take your most repeated tasks today?
**Plan:**
1. Track two weeks of time by Doing, Deciding, Delegating, Designing [G17].
2. Name the Queen Bee Role and make sure someone other than you protects it [G17].
3. Capture your repeated tasks as recorded systems [G17, G02].
4. Draw an org chart of positions and mark the boxes with your name [G02, G03].
5. Hand off the first box with clear outcomes and intent, managing closely at first [G19, G29].
6. Where a task repeats, consider software or content instead of a hire [G30].
7. Block a weekly Designing session; hand off time habits to Productivity if needed.
**Metric to watch:** hours per week the owner spends Designing.
**Common mistake:** delegating tasks without the outcome or system, then taking them back.

## 3. "We're profitable but always out of cash"
**Ask first:**
- How many days from paying suppliers or staff to being paid by customers?
- Do customers pay deposits or up front?
- How much of profit goes to debt, owner draws or tax surprises?
- Is growth itself consuming the cash?
**Plan:**
1. Measure the Cash Conversion Cycle [G04].
2. Run the Power of One on price, receivable days and payable days [G04].
3. Introduce deposits, up-front billing or recurring payment where possible [G16, G04].
4. Set up Profit First accounts, including a Tax account [G10].
5. Allocate on a fixed rhythm and review the Opex account for cuts [G10, G27].
6. Build a cash reserve of a set number of months of costs [G28].
7. Check with an accountant before changing tax or debt structure.
**Metric to watch:** cash conversion cycle in days, and month-end cash balance.
**Common mistake:** chasing more sales, which makes a long cash cycle worse.

## 4. "I want to sell the business in 3 to 5 years"
**Ask first:**
- Could the business run for three months without you?
- How much revenue is recurring or contracted?
- Who sells, and who manages delivery?
- What would a buyer see as the biggest risk?
**Plan:**
1. Score Built to Sell Readiness [G16].
2. Standardize the main offer and stop owner-dependent custom work [G16, G02].
3. Shift clients to recurring or up-front billing [G16].
4. Build the Accountability Chart and fill management seats [G03, G24].
5. Give key managers long-term incentives [G16].
6. Run clean weekly numbers a buyer can trust [G03, G04].
7. Prove it with an extended owner absence [G16, G17].
**Metric to watch:** share of revenue that arrives without the owner selling or delivering.
**Common mistake:** waiting until a year before sale to reduce owner dependence.

## 5. "I keep hiring the wrong people"
**Ask first:**
- Is there a written scorecard of outcomes for the role?
- How do candidates come to you?
- Do you check references in depth?
- Was the seat itself clearly defined?
**Plan:**
1. Define the seat on the Accountability Chart first [G03].
2. Write a scorecard: mission, outcomes, competencies [G24].
3. Source through referrals from staff and network [G24].
4. Run a structured career-history interview [G24].
5. Make reference calls and tell candidates you will [G24].
6. Sell the role on what matters to the candidate [G24].
7. Review fit at 90 days against values and outcomes [G03, G01].
**Metric to watch:** share of hires still in seat and meeting scorecard outcomes after a year.
**Common mistake:** hiring the most likeable candidate under time pressure.

## 6. "My leadership team doesn't work together"
**Ask first:**
- Do leaders admit mistakes to each other?
- Do people argue in meetings or only afterward?
- Do decisions stick or get reopened?
- Who holds peers to account, apart from you?
**Plan:**
1. Diagnose with the Five Dysfunctions pyramid; start at the lowest broken level [G25].
2. Owner goes first in admitting a mistake and owning team failures [G25, G29].
3. Set a weekly meeting with a fixed agenda and IDS [G03].
4. Let everyone weigh in, then close each decision clearly; no consensus needed [G19, G25].
5. Log decisions and owners [G03].
6. Make team results the shared scorecard [G25, G03].
**Metric to watch:** share of meeting decisions completed on time without being reopened.
**Common mistake:** team-building events without changing how meetings and decisions work.

## 7. "Everything is a priority and nothing gets done"
**Ask first:**
- How many active projects are there right now?
- Who owns each one?
- What is the one result that matters most this quarter?
- How often is progress reviewed?
**Plan:**
1. List all projects and cut to three to seven company Rocks [G03, G04].
2. Give each one owner [G03].
3. Link each to the constraint or the strategy kernel; if overwhelmed, rank and work one at a time [G23, G14, G29].
4. Use OKRs only if the team is large enough to need alignment [G12].
5. Review on or off track weekly [G03].
6. Write a stop-doing list [G01].
**Metric to watch:** share of quarterly priorities completed.
**Common mistake:** adding priorities mid-quarter without dropping any.

## 8. "Should we enter a new market or launch a new product?"
**Ask first:**
- Does it fit what you are best at and your economic engine?
- Who already holds that ground, and where are they weak?
- What is the cheapest real test?
- Who will run it, and what does it pull from the core?
**Plan:**
1. Check the Hedgehog Concept fit [G01].
2. Compare your position with incumbents on the Five Factors; enter only where you win [G26].
3. Pick one narrow beachhead segment [G13].
4. Test with an MVP using real money or commitments [G05].
5. Put a proven person on it before scaling [G01].
6. Set a pivot or persevere date [G05].
**Metric to watch:** paying customers or pre-orders from the test segment.
**Common mistake:** launching fully because the idea is exciting, starving the core.

## 9. "A cheaper competitor is taking our low end"
**Ask first:**
- Which customers are leaving, and are they your most or least profitable?
- Is the competitor "good enough" for them?
- Are you over-serving some customers?
- Could you run a cheaper offer with separate costs?
**Plan:**
1. Map who is leaving and why [G09].
2. Draw the Strategy Canvas for you and the rival [G08].
3. Decide: let them go, answer with a separate low-end offer, or redraw your factors; do not fight on their strong ground [G09, G08, G26].
4. If launching a low-end offer, give it its own team and economics [G09].
5. Strengthen value for the customers you keep with the ERRC grid [G08].
**Metric to watch:** gross margin and retention in your core segment.
**Common mistake:** cutting prices across the board, starting a long price war that drains cash [G26].

## 10. "Should I buy a business or start one?"
**Ask first:**
- Do you want cash flow within a year?
- Are you an operator or an inventor?
- How much debt could you carry through a bad year?
- Does the target run without its owner?
**Plan:**
1. Judge the target as a buyer would: standard offer, recurring revenue, many customers, a team that runs it [G16].
2. Apply the asset test: will it put cash in your pocket after debt and your own pay [G27]?
3. Stress test debt against a revenue drop; leave room for error [G28].
4. Do full due diligence with an accountant and attorney.
5. If starting, test demand cheaply first [G05].
6. Either way, plan for owner independence from day one [G02, G16].
**Metric to watch:** cash left after debt payments and owner pay (buy), or paying customers (start).
**Common mistake:** buying a job with so much debt that one bad year sinks it.

## 11. "Should we raise money and grow fast?"
**Ask first:**
- Does scale decide who wins this market (network effects, big fixed costs)?
- Do you have product/market fit evidence?
- What do you lose by growing slowly?
- Can the team and systems survive doubling?
**Plan:**
1. Run the Monopoly Test: is there a market you can dominate [G06]?
2. Check the Engines of Growth and unit economics [G05].
3. Aim one round of money at one segment, not many [G13].
4. If conditions fail, grow on profit and keep a reserve [G10, G18, G28].
5. If they hold, rebuild management as headcount grows [G19, G03].
**Metric to watch:** months of runway and the core engine metric.
**Common mistake:** raising money to fix a product/market fit problem.

## 12. "Margins are shrinking as we grow"
**Ask first:**
- Which customers or offers lost margin?
- Has custom work crept in?
- Are new overheads tied to growth or to habit?
- When did you last raise prices?
**Plan:**
1. Run the Power of One [G04].
2. Rank customers and offers by profit [G01, G04].
3. Standardize scope and stop custom work [G16, G02].
4. Use Profit First to force cost discipline [G10].
5. Hand off pricing tests to Sales if needed.
**Metric to watch:** gross margin by offer, monthly.
**Common mistake:** assuming volume will fix margin.

## 13. "Early customers loved it, now growth stalled"
**Ask first:**
- Who were your first buyers: enthusiasts or pragmatists?
- Do new prospects ask for references in their industry?
- Is anything missing for customers to succeed?
- How many segments do you sell to?
**Plan:**
1. Place buyers on the Technology Adoption Life Cycle [G13].
2. Pick one beachhead segment [G13].
3. Build the Whole Product for them [G13].
4. Win references there [G13].
5. Expand to adjacent segments only after leadership in the first [G13, G06].
**Metric to watch:** share of target segment won, and reference customers.
**Common mistake:** selling to every segment at once.

## 14. "We are in crisis and may need to cut people"
**Ask first:**
- How many months of cash remain?
- What is the true cause: market, product, cost?
- What must be protected to survive?
- Has the team been told the facts?
**Plan:**
1. Face the facts plainly [G01, G11].
2. Switch to wartime focus: rank problems and work the top one; make fast calls [G11, G29].
3. Cut once, deeply enough, rather than several times [G11].
4. Lay people off with respect; leaders deliver the news [G11].
5. Protect the constraint and the core offer [G23, G01].
6. Track cash weekly [G04, G10].
**Metric to watch:** weekly cash and months of runway.
**Common mistake:** waiting and cutting in small rounds that wreck morale.

## 15. "I don't know which numbers to watch"
**Ask first:**
- What would tell you on Monday that the week will go badly?
- What is your economic engine ratio?
- Who owns each number?
- How often do you see your numbers now?
**Plan:**
1. Find your profit per X [G01].
2. Choose 5 to 15 weekly numbers, mostly leading [G03, G19].
3. Pair numbers to stop gaming [G19].
4. Give each an owner and goal [G03].
5. Review weekly in the team meeting [G03, G04].
6. Avoid vanity totals [G05].
**Metric to watch:** the economic engine ratio.
**Common mistake:** tracking too many numbers with nobody owning them.

## 16. "Our strategy is just a set of goals"
**Ask first:**
- What is the main obstacle to winning?
- Where do you choose not to play?
- How do you win: lower cost or real difference?
- Do the planned actions support each other?
**Plan:**
1. Write the Strategy Kernel diagnosis [G14].
2. Compare your position with the main rival on the Five Factors [G26].
3. Check the Hedgehog fit [G01].
4. Consider an ERRC grid if the industry is commoditized [G08].
5. Set guiding policy and three coherent actions [G14].
6. Turn actions into Rocks [G03].
**Metric to watch:** share of resources going to the chosen actions.
**Common mistake:** mistaking a revenue target for a strategy.

## 17. "How big should this business get?"
**Ask first:**
- What income, hours and role do you want in five years?
- Has the target moved each time you hit it?
- How many months could you survive a 30% revenue drop?
- What do you own that earns without your hours?
**Plan:**
1. Write the Primary Aim and your number for enough [G02, G28].
2. Set a cash reserve and debt ceiling before any expansion [G28, G10].
3. Test growth ideas against enough: does this move get you there or move the goalposts [G28, G18]?
4. Grow leverage before headcount: productized offers, software, content [G30, G16].
5. Review enough once a year, not at every milestone [G28].
**Metric to watch:** owner hours per week against owner pay and months of reserve.
**Common mistake:** chasing a bigger number that adds stress and risk but not the life the owner wants.
