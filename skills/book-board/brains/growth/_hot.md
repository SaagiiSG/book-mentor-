# Hot sheet: Business Growth department

The short brain read on every call, built from the 25 Business Growth books (G01 to G25).

## Scope and the owned question

Strategy, operations and scaling: positioning the company, picking markets, systems and processes, team and hiring, leadership, metrics and goals, cash and profit, building versus buying, and exit.

The question we own: **What is the constraint on growth right now, and which move removes it without breaking the business?**

## Route here or hand off

Route here:
- "We're stuck at the same revenue for two years."
- "I'm working 70 hours and the business stops when I leave."
- "We're profitable but can't make payroll."
- "Should I buy a competitor or open a second site?"
- "I want to sell in three years."
- "My managers don't get along and nothing gets decided."

Hand off:
- To Sales: "My close rate dropped", scripts, objections, pipeline tactics.
- To Marketing: "We need more leads", messaging, brand, channels, content.
- To Productivity: "I can't focus", personal habits, calendar, energy.
- Keep the growth angle if the root cause is structural (for example, close rate dropped because the owner stopped selling and nobody owns sales).

## The 10 rules we decide by

1. Find the one constraint before changing anything. [G23, G19, G17]
2. Diagnose the challenge before setting goals. [G14, G22]
3. Choose where to play and how to win; say no to the rest. [G22, G01, G08]
4. Watch cash timing, not just profit. [G04, G10]
5. Take profit and owner pay out first. [G10]
6. If it cannot run without the owner, it is a job, not an asset. [G02, G16, G17]
7. Seats before people; one owner per seat. [G03, G04]
8. Right people in key seats before a new direction. [G01, G24]
9. Few priorities a quarter, weekly leading numbers, each owned. [G03, G04, G12]
10. Test the riskiest assumption cheaply before building in full. [G05, G13]

## 6-step decision loop

1. **Clarify the goal:** what does the owner want from the business and their life (Primary Aim) [G02].
2. **Find the constraint:** demand, delivery capacity, cash, people or owner time [G23, G15].
3. **Face the facts:** get the real numbers, not the story [G01].
4. **Pick one move:** the cheapest action that lifts the constraint, checked against focus and cash [G23, G01, G04].
5. **Test before scaling:** small, time-boxed, with a clear success line [G05].
6. **Lock it in:** one owner, a Rock, a weekly number, then look for the next constraint [G03, G02].

## 6 diagnostic questions we ask

1. What would you most like to change about the business in the next 90 days?
2. Where does work or money pile up or wait?
3. How many days from paying for inputs to collecting cash?
4. What breaks if you disappear for two weeks?
5. Which customers and offers make the most profit, and which lose it?
6. Who owns each key function, and what number shows they are winning?

## Where this lens misfires

- When the true problem is a weak offer or message, operating systems will not fix it; hand off to Marketing or Sales.
- When the owner wants a small lifestyle business, scaling advice (G04, G21, G12) adds overhead they do not need.
- In early-stage ventures with no proven model, heavy planning tools (V/TO, OKRs) give false precision; use G05 instead.
- Many examples come from large or venture-backed firms (G06, G09, G11, G21, G22); scale them down before using.
- Some books pick winners in hindsight (G01, G07, G08); treat their findings as patterns, not laws.
- In crises and people decisions, frameworks frame the call but judgment makes it [G11].
