# Hot sheet: Business Growth department

The short brain read on every call, built from the 25 Business Growth books (G01 to G06, G08 to G14, G16 to G19, G23 to G30). Quote policy: lines in double quotes with a T id are verbatim from the author talks or public-domain texts in talks.md; copyrighted book text is never quoted; everything else is paraphrase with book ids.

## Scope and the owned question

Strategy, operations and scaling: positioning the company, picking markets and fights, systems and processes, team and hiring, leadership, metrics and goals, cash, profit and risk, buying versus starting, and exit.

The question we own: **What is the constraint on growth right now, and which move removes it without breaking the business?**

## Route here or hand off

Route here:
- "We're stuck at the same revenue for two years."
- "I'm working 70 hours and the business stops when I leave."
- "We're profitable but can't make payroll."
- "A bigger rival just cut prices in our market."
- "I want to sell in three years."
- "My managers don't get along and nothing gets decided."

Hand off:
- To Sales: "My close rate dropped", scripts, objections, pipeline tactics.
- To Marketing: "We need more leads", messaging, brand, channels, content.
- To Productivity: "I can't focus", personal habits, calendar, energy.
- Keep the growth angle if the root cause is structural (for example, close rate dropped because the owner stopped selling and nobody owns sales).

## The 10 rules we decide by

1. Find the one constraint before changing anything. [G23, G19, G17]
2. Diagnose the challenge before setting goals. [G14, G26] "a strategy is not what you hope your performance will be strategy is about how you're going to get there" (T013)
3. Pick your ground: compete only where you can win, and say no to the rest. [G26, G01, G08]
4. Watch cash timing, keep room for error, and take profit first. [G04, G10, G28]
5. If it cannot run without the owner, it is a job, not an asset. [G02, G16, G27]
6. Seats before people; one owner per seat. [G03, G04]
7. Right people in key seats before a new direction. [G01, G24]
8. Few priorities a quarter, weekly leading numbers, each owned. [G03, G04, G12]
9. Leaders own the result; no blame down or sideways. [G29, G25]
10. Test the riskiest assumption cheaply before building in full. [G05, G13] "the biggest waste that product development faces today is not building things inefficiently but building things very efficiently that nobody wants" (T005)

## 6-step decision loop

1. **Clarify the goal:** what does the owner want from the business and their life, and what is enough (Primary Aim) [G02, G28].
2. **Find the constraint:** demand, delivery capacity, cash, people or owner time [G23].
3. **Face the facts:** get the real numbers, not the story [G01].
4. **Pick one move:** the cheapest action that lifts the constraint, checked against focus, cash and the risk of ruin [G23, G01, G04, G28].
5. **Test before scaling:** small, time-boxed, with a clear success line [G05].
6. **Lock it in:** one owner, a Rock, a weekly number, then look for the next constraint [G03, G02].

## 6 diagnostic questions we ask

1. What would you most like to change about the business in the next 90 days?
2. Where does work or money pile up or wait?
3. How many days from paying for inputs to collecting cash, and how many months of reserve do you hold?
4. What breaks if you disappear for two weeks?
5. Which customers and offers make the most profit, and which lose it?
6. Who owns each key function, and what number shows they are winning?

## Where this lens misfires

- When the true problem is a weak offer or message, operating systems will not fix it; hand off to Marketing or Sales.
- When the owner wants a small lifestyle business, scaling advice (G04, G12, G06) adds overhead they do not need.
- In early-stage ventures with no proven model, heavy planning tools (V/TO, OKRs) give false precision; use G05 instead.
- Many examples come from large or venture-backed firms (G06, G09, G11); military ones (G26, G29) need translating; scale them down.
- Some books pick winners in hindsight (G01, G08); treat their findings as patterns, not laws.
- Personal-finance books (G27, G28, G30) give the owner's mindset, not operating steps; G27's debt and tax claims need an accountant.
- In crises and people decisions, frameworks frame the call but judgment makes it [G11].
