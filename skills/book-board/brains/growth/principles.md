# Principles: Business Growth department

Decision rules built from the 25 Business Growth books (G01 to G25), paraphrased, each with its sources and how many books support it.

## Decision rules

1. Find the one constraint on growth before you change anything else. [G23, G19, G17, G04] Confidence: high, four books agree.
2. Improving a part of the business that is not the constraint mostly wastes effort. [G23, G19] Confidence: medium, two books, strong logic.
3. Strategy starts with a diagnosis of the main challenge, not with a revenue goal. [G14, G22] Confidence: medium, two books agree, synthesis of [G14, G22].
4. Choose where you will play and how you will win, and say no to the rest. [G22, G14, G01, G08] Confidence: high, four books.
5. Focus on what you can be best at and what drives your economics, and stop doing the rest. [G01, G18, G16] Confidence: high, three books.
6. Take profit and owner pay out first, then run the business on what is left. [G10] Confidence: low to medium, one book; accountants differ.
7. Watch cash timing, not just profit: a growing business can run out of cash while profitable. [G04, G10, G15] Confidence: high, three books.
8. Pull the small levers first: price, collection days and costs often beat chasing more volume. [G04, G15] Confidence: medium, two books.
9. If the business cannot run without the owner, the owner has a job, not an asset. [G02, G16, G17, G07] Confidence: high, four books.
10. Write down how work is done, measure it, then make the better way the standard. [G02, G17, G03] Confidence: high, three books.
11. Define the seats before choosing people, and give each seat one owner. [G03, G04, G02] Confidence: high, three books.
12. Get the right people in key seats before committing to a new direction. [G01, G24, G04] Confidence: high, three books.
13. Hire against a written scorecard of outcomes, not on gut feel. [G24, G04] Confidence: medium, two books.
14. When in doubt about a hire, do not hire; hire when the pain is real. [G01, G18] Confidence: medium, two books.
15. Act fast on a wrong person in a key seat, and treat them with respect. [G01, G11, G03] Confidence: high, three books.
16. Pick a few priorities per quarter, each with one owner. [G03, G04, G12] Confidence: high, three books.
17. Track a short weekly set of leading numbers, each with an owner. [G03, G04, G19, G05] Confidence: high, four books.
18. Pair metrics so improving one does not quietly damage another. [G19, G05] Confidence: medium, two books.
19. Ignore vanity numbers; judge progress by cohort, conversion and retention. [G05] Confidence: medium, one book, widely accepted.
20. Hold a fixed meeting rhythm and solve issues at the root, not by discussing them weekly. [G03, G04, G19] Confidence: high, three books.
21. A leadership team needs trust and open debate before it can commit and hold each other to account. [G25, G11] Confidence: medium, two books.
22. Disagree in the room, then fully support the decision once made. [G19, G25] Confidence: medium, two books.
23. A manager's job is the team's output, so spend time where it multiplies others' work. [G19, G11] Confidence: medium, two books.
24. Manage closely on tasks people are new to, and loosely where they are proven. [G19] Confidence: low to medium, one book.
25. Test the riskiest assumption cheaply with real customer behavior before building in full. [G05, G18] Confidence: medium, two books.
26. Set a review date to decide pivot or persevere; do not drift. [G05, G14] Confidence: medium, synthesis of [G05, G14].
27. Win one narrow segment completely before expanding. [G13, G06, G22] Confidence: high, three books.
28. Mainstream buyers need the whole solution and references from peers. [G13] Confidence: medium, one book, widely used in B2B.
29. In a commodity market, change the factors you compete on rather than fighting on price. [G08, G06] Confidence: medium, two books.
30. Take cheap "good enough" rivals at your low end seriously, and answer with separate economics. [G09] Confidence: medium, one book, contested.
31. Keep the core values and purpose fixed and change everything else. [G07, G01, G03] Confidence: high, three books.
32. Keep pushing one coherent direction; constant new programs reset progress. [G01, G14] Confidence: medium, two books.
33. Face the worst facts honestly while staying sure you will get through. [G01, G11] Confidence: medium, two books.
34. Match leadership style to the threat: broad in peace, focused and directive in a crisis. [G11] Confidence: low to medium, one book.
35. Avoid quick people fixes that create management debt. [G11] Confidence: low to medium, one book.
36. Before starting from zero, compare the risk of buying an existing profitable business. [G20] Confidence: low to medium, one book.
37. Speed over efficiency only pays in winner-take-most markets with capital behind you. [G21, G18, G10] Confidence: medium, synthesis of [G21, G18, G10]; G21 argues for speed, G18 and G10 for restraint.
38. Recurring revenue, standard offers and up-front payment raise both cash and business value. [G16, G04] Confidence: medium, two books.

## Where the books disagree

**1. Grow fast versus stay lean: Blitzscaling [G21] versus Rework [G18] and Profit First [G10].** G21 says accept waste to win the market first. G18 and G10 say stay small, profitable and calm. G21 is right in winner-take-most markets with network effects and capital. G18 and G10 are right for most owner-funded firms, where a cash crunch kills the business before scale arrives.

**2. Plan versus adapt: EOS and OKRs [G03, G12, G04] versus Rework [G18] and Lean Startup [G05].** G03, G04 and G12 set 1, 3 and 10-year targets and quarterly goals. G18 treats long plans as guesses; G05 treats plans as hypotheses. Planning wins when the business model is proven and the problem is execution. Adapting wins with a new product or market where the facts are unknown.

**3. Iterate versus bet on a definite vision: Lean Startup [G05] versus Zero to One [G06].** G05 says learn fast through small tests. G06 favors a definite long-term plan built on a secret and warns against incremental tinkering. Iterate when you can test cheaply and the market will tell you. Bet when the opportunity needs a large, coordinated build that small tests cannot reveal.

**4. Listen to customers versus customers can mislead: Lean Startup [G05] and Crossing the Chasm [G13] versus The Innovator's Dilemma [G09].** G05 and G13 tie success to customer evidence. G09 shows that serving your best current customers well can blind you to disruption. Listen to customers to improve your current offer. Look at noncustomers and the low end when deciding on new markets or threats.

**5. Systems over people versus people over systems: E-Myth [G02] and Clockwork [G17] versus Good to Great [G01] and Who [G24].** G02 wants systems ordinary people can run. G01 and G24 put getting exceptional people first. Systems are right for repeatable delivery work. People come first for leadership and judgment roles where systems cannot cover every case.

**6. Recipes versus no recipe: Traction [G03] and Scaling Up [G04] versus The Hard Thing About Hard Things [G11].** G03 and G04 offer complete operating systems. G11 says the hardest calls have no formula. Use the systems for running the business week to week. In crises, layoffs and leadership changes, use judgment; a framework only frames the call.

**7. Focus narrow versus create new space: Built to Sell [G16] and Hedgehog [G01] versus Blue Ocean [G08].** G16 and G01 say specialize in what you already do best. G08 says redraw the industry's factors and reach noncustomers. Narrow focus is right when you have an edge but spread it thin. Blue ocean thinking is right when your whole industry is commoditized and focus alone still leaves you in a price war.

**8. Owner hands off versus manager stays close: Clockwork [G17] versus High Output Management [G19].** G17 aims for an owner free from daily work. G19 says managers should intervene closely where people are inexperienced. Hand off where people are proven and systems exist. Stay close during new hires, new roles and crises, then step back.

**9. Build versus buy: Lean Startup [G05] and Zero to One [G06] versus Buy Then Build [G20].** G05 and G06 assume you build. G20 argues buying a proven business removes startup risk. Build when the idea is new or the market does not yet exist. Buy when you want cash flow soon, have operator skills, and can find a fair deal.

**10. Profit now versus invest now: Profit First [G10] versus Blitzscaling [G21] and Zero to One [G06].** G10 takes profit from day one. G21 and G06 accept losses to build a dominant position. Profit first is right for owner-funded firms and anyone whose cash habits are loose. Investing ahead is right only with outside capital and a market that rewards scale.
