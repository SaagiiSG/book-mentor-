# Principles: Business Growth department

Decision rules built from the 25 Business Growth books (G01 to G06, G08 to G14, G16 to G19, G23 to G30), paraphrased, each with its sources and how many books support it. Quote policy: verbatim quotes only from the author talks and public-domain texts in talks.md, with a T id; copyrighted book text is never quoted.

## Decision rules

1. Find the one constraint on growth before you change anything else. [G23, G19, G17, G04] Confidence: high, four books agree.
2. Improving a part of the business that is not the constraint mostly wastes effort. [G23, G19] Confidence: medium, two books, strong logic.
3. Strategy starts with a diagnosis of the main challenge, not with a revenue goal. [G14, G26] Confidence: medium, two books agree.
4. Choose where you will compete and how you will win, and say no to the rest. [G14, G01, G08, G26] Confidence: high, four books.
5. Focus on what you can be best at and what drives your economics, and stop doing the rest. [G01, G18, G16] Confidence: high, three books.
6. Take profit and owner pay out first, then run the business on what is left. [G10] Confidence: low to medium, one book; accountants differ.
7. Watch cash timing, not just profit: a growing business can run out of cash while profitable. [G04, G10, G16] Confidence: high, three books.
8. Pull the small levers first: price, collection days and costs often beat chasing more volume. [G04, G18] Confidence: medium, two books.
9. If the business cannot run without the owner, the owner has a job, not an asset. [G02, G16, G17, G27] Confidence: high, four books.
10. Write down how work is done, measure it, then make the better way the standard. [G02, G17, G03] Confidence: high, three books.
11. Define the seats before choosing people, and give each seat one owner. [G03, G04, G02] Confidence: high, three books.
12. Get the right people in key seats before committing to a new direction. [G01, G24, G04] Confidence: high, three books.
13. Hire against a written scorecard of outcomes with numbers, not on gut feel. [G24, G04] Confidence: medium, two books.
14. When in doubt about a hire, do not hire; hire when the pain is real. [G01, G18] Confidence: medium, two books.
15. Act fast on a wrong person in a key seat, and treat them with respect. [G01, G11, G03] Confidence: high, three books.
16. Pick a few priorities per quarter, each with one owner. [G03, G04, G12, G29] Confidence: high, four books.
17. Track a short weekly set of leading numbers, each with an owner. [G03, G04, G19, G05] Confidence: high, four books.
18. Pair metrics so improving one does not quietly damage another. [G19, G05, G12] Confidence: medium, three books.
19. Ignore vanity numbers; judge progress by cohort, conversion and retention. [G05] Confidence: medium, one book, widely accepted.
20. Hold a fixed meeting rhythm and solve issues at the root, not by discussing them weekly. [G03, G04, G19] Confidence: high, three books.
21. A leadership team needs trust and open debate before it can commit and hold each other to account. [G25, G11] Confidence: medium, two books.
22. Let everyone weigh in, then decide and fully support the decision; consensus is not required. [G19, G25] Confidence: medium, two books.
23. A manager's job is the team's output, so spend time where it multiplies others' work. [G19, G11] Confidence: medium, two books.
24. Manage closely on tasks people are new to, and loosely where they are proven. [G19, G29] Confidence: medium, two books.
25. Test the riskiest assumption cheaply with real customer behavior before building in full. [G05, G18] Confidence: medium, two books.
26. Set a review date to decide pivot or persevere; do not drift. [G05, G14] Confidence: medium, synthesis of [G05, G14].
27. Win one narrow segment completely before expanding. [G13, G06] Confidence: medium, two books.
28. Mainstream buyers need the whole solution and references from peers. [G13] Confidence: medium, one book, widely used in B2B.
29. In a commodity market, change the factors you compete on rather than fighting on price. [G08, G06, G26] Confidence: high, three books.
30. Take cheap "good enough" rivals at your low end seriously, and answer with separate economics. [G09] Confidence: medium, one book, contested.
31. Keep the core values and focus fixed and change everything else. [G03, G04] Confidence: medium, two books.
32. Keep pushing one coherent direction; constant new programs reset progress. [G01, G14] Confidence: medium, two books.
33. Face the worst facts honestly while staying sure you will get through; bad news must reach the top fast. [G01, G11] Confidence: medium, two books.
34. Match leadership style to the threat: broad in peace, focused in a crisis. [G11, G29] Confidence: medium, two books.
35. Avoid quick people fixes and avoided decisions; both pile up as debt. [G11] Confidence: low to medium, one book.
36. Recurring revenue, standard offers and up-front payment raise both cash and business value. [G16, G04] Confidence: medium, two books.
37. The leader owns every failure in their area; fix your part before blaming the team. [G29, G25, G02] Confidence: medium, three books.
38. Compare your position with the rival's before you fight, and fight only where you already win. [G26, G14] Confidence: medium, two books.
39. Keep fights short: long price wars and drawn-out campaigns drain cash. [G26, G10] Confidence: medium, two books.
40. Judge spending by whether it puts cash in your pocket; status purchases are liabilities. [G27, G10, G18] Confidence: medium, three books.
41. Keep room for error: a cash reserve and debt you can survive a bad year with. [G28, G10] Confidence: medium, two books.
42. Decide what enough is before chasing growth; moving goalposts burn owners out. [G28, G18, G02] Confidence: medium, three books.
43. Prefer leverage that needs no one's permission (software, content) over adding headcount where it fits. [G30, G18] Confidence: low to medium, two books.
44. Play long games: compounding in skills, customers and reputation takes years. [G30, G28, G01] Confidence: high, three books.

## Where the books disagree

**1. Grow fast versus stay lean: Zero to One [G06] versus Rework [G18], Profit First [G10] and The Psychology of Money [G28].** G06 accepts years of losses to build a dominant position. G18 and G10 say stay small and profitable from day one; G28 says avoid any risk of ruin. G06 fits outside capital and a market where scale wins; the others fit most owner-funded firms, where a cash crunch kills the business first.

**2. Plan versus adapt: EOS and OKRs [G03, G12, G04] versus Rework [G18] and Lean Startup [G05].** G03, G04 and G12 set long-range targets and quarterly goals; G18 calls long plans guesses and G05 treats them as hypotheses. Planning wins when the business model is proven and the problem is execution. Adapting wins with a new product or market where the facts are unknown.

**3. Iterate versus bet on a definite vision: Lean Startup [G05] versus Zero to One [G06].** G05 says learn fast through small tests. G06 favors a definite long-term plan built on a secret and warns against incremental tinkering. Iterate when tests are cheap; bet when the opportunity needs a large build small tests cannot reveal.

**4. Listen to customers versus customers can mislead: Lean Startup [G05] and Crossing the Chasm [G13] versus The Innovator's Dilemma [G09].** G05 and G13 tie success to customer evidence; G09 shows serving your best customers can blind you to disruption. Listen to customers to improve the current offer; look at noncustomers and the low end for new markets and threats.

**5. Systems over people versus people over systems: E-Myth [G02] and Clockwork [G17] versus Good to Great [G01] and Who [G24].** G02 wants systems ordinary people can run; G01 and G24 put exceptional people first. Systems are right for repeatable delivery work. People come first for leadership and judgment roles where systems cannot cover every case.

**6. Recipes versus no recipe: Traction [G03] and Scaling Up [G04] versus The Hard Thing About Hard Things [G11].** G03 and G04 offer complete operating systems. G11 says the hardest calls have no formula. Use the systems week to week; in crises and leadership changes, judgment decides.

**7. Focus narrow versus create new space: Built to Sell [G16] and Hedgehog [G01] versus Blue Ocean [G08].** G16 and G01 say specialize; G08 says redraw the industry's factors and reach noncustomers. Narrow focus fits when you have an edge but spread it thin; blue ocean fits when the whole industry is commoditized.

**8. Owner hands off versus manager stays close: Clockwork [G17] versus High Output Management [G19].** G17 aims for an owner free from daily work; G19 says intervene closely where people are inexperienced. Hand off where people are proven and systems exist. Stay close during new hires, new roles and crises, then step back.

**9. Study the rival versus ignore the rival: The Art of War [G26] versus Rework [G18] and Blue Ocean [G08].** G26 says know the opponent as well as yourself and pick your ground against them. G18 says your real competition is your own costs; G08 says stop benchmarking rivals and create new demand. Study the rival when one attacks you or you enter their ground; otherwise spend the effort on costs and noncustomers.

**10. Use debt to build assets versus keep room for error: Rich Dad Poor Dad [G27] versus The Psychology of Money [G28] and Profit First [G10].** G27 treats debt as a tool to buy cash-flowing assets. G28 puts survival first and G10 builds cash before spending. Borrow only for an asset with proven cash flow that still covers payments in a bad year. Otherwise keep the buffer.

**11. Central control in a crisis versus push decisions down: The Hard Thing About Hard Things [G11] versus Extreme Ownership [G29].** G11's wartime CEO narrows focus and directs closely. G29 says even in combat, leaders should give clear intent and let junior leaders decide. Centralize the few survival decisions (cash, cuts, the main priority). Push the rest down with clear intent so the owner is not the new bottleneck.

**12. Owner's knowledge is the product versus the business must not need the owner: The Almanack of Naval Ravikant [G30] versus E-Myth [G02] and Built to Sell [G16].** G30 builds wealth on the owner's specific knowledge and leverage. G02 and G16 say a business that needs the owner is a job and cannot be sold. Lean on specific knowledge when the goal is income and freedom and it can be leveraged through software or content. Systemize away from the owner when the goal is a sale.
