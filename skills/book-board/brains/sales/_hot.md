# Hot sheet: Sales department

Built from the 25 Sales books in BOOKS.md (S01 to S28, retired ids skipped). Read on every call before any other brain file.

Quote policy: verbatim quotes appear only from the author talks and public-domain texts listed in talks.md, always with a T id. Text of copyrighted books is never quoted. Everything else is paraphrase cited by book id.

## Scope and the owned question

This department owns selling and closing: prospecting, discovery, qualification, pitching, negotiation, objections, pricing conversations, offer construction for the sale, the sales process, and sales team management.

The question it owns: **"Will this close, at what price, and what exactly do you say and do to get the yes?"**

## Route here, or hand off

Route here:
- "My close rate dropped from 30 to 18 percent."
- "Buyers say we're too expensive. What do I say?"
- "How do I negotiate this contract renewal?"
- "Should I hire my first salesperson, and how do I pay them?"
- "Deals stall after the proposal."

Hand off:
- "Nobody knows who we are" or "what should our ads say": hand off to Marketing.
- "Which niche should we position for": hand off to Marketing (Sales can say which segment closes best).
- "Should we expand, raise money, or fix our margins": hand off to Growth.
- "My team is dysfunctional" beyond sales roles: hand off to Growth.
- "I can't find time to sell": hand off to Productivity for the calendar, keep the selling plan here.

## The 10 rules

1. Diagnose before you prescribe; no pitch until the buyer states the problem and its cost. [S01, S11, S12, S25] As Rackham found, "top salespeople didn't talk about solutions until very late in the sales process" (T075).
2. People buy change, not products; sell the gap, and the impact is what moves them. [S11, S02] "impact is where the motivation to buy exists" (T085).
3. Every meeting must end with a dated next step on both calendars. [S01, S22]
4. Qualify out early; a slow no costs more than a fast one. [S09, S03, S06]
5. Fix the offer before the script when every rep struggles. [S07]
6. Raise value and lower risk before you cut price; when they say no to price, offer a planned downsell, not a discount. [S07, S28, S15]
7. If the buyer agrees but stalls, it is low certainty: recommend one path and lower the step to act. [S15, S19]
8. Empathy and calibrated questions beat arguing; never attack the buyer's past choice. [S03, S04, S27]
9. Read the power around the deal and make your contact look good, but stay candid. [S26, S25]
10. Prospect daily; neediness kills deals. [S10, S06, S18]

## The 6-step decision loop

1. **Locate the break.** Which stage fails: pipeline, discovery, proposal, negotiation, close, or retention?
2. **Name the barrier.** No impact, no access to the signer, weak offer, low certainty, status quo, or internal politics.
3. **Pick the framework** that fits that barrier (see the map below).
4. **Score it** with the 1 to 5 guide in frameworks.md.
5. **Write the words and actions** for the next conversation: the question to ask, the offer change, the advance to request.
6. **Set one metric** and a date to recheck; adjust the next week.

## 6 diagnostic questions

1. Where in the funnel do deals die, and has that changed?
2. What problem does the buyer say they have, and what does it cost them, in their words?
3. Who signs, and have you met them?
4. Are you losing to competitors, or to "no decision"?
5. What do your best customers have in common, and what do they buy after the first sale?
6. How many new qualified conversations start each week?

## Where this lens misfires

- **When the real problem is demand or positioning.** If nobody is entering the funnel or the market does not know you, more sales technique will not help. Hand off to Marketing.
- **When delivery fails.** Better closing or more upsells on a product that disappoints make churn and reputation worse [S28]. Hand off to Growth.
- **When frameworks assume scale.** Role specialization and data-driven hiring [S09, S08] assume teams and volume. A solo owner needs light versions.
- **When pressure or power tactics leak in.** Closing scripts [S15, S23, S21] and power moves [S26] can harm trust in small, connected markets and in advisory work [S25, S13]. Greene himself says "the laws are never meant to apply to every situation" (T099).
- **When the founder is the bottleneck for time, not skill.** Selling plans fail without protected hours. Hand off to Productivity.
- **When the research base is thin.** Some books rest on one company's data or single studies [S02, S08, S13], and some on the author's own businesses [S07, S28]; treat specific findings as directional.
