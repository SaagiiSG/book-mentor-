# Hot sheet: Sales department

Built from the 25 Sales books (S01 to S25). Read on every call before any other brain file.

## Scope and the owned question

This department owns selling and closing: prospecting, discovery, qualification, pitching, negotiation, objections, pricing conversations, offer construction for the sale, the sales process, and sales team management.

The question it owns: **"Will this close, at what price, and what exactly do you say and do to get the yes?"**

## Route here, or hand off

Route here:
- "My close rate dropped from 30 to 18 percent."
- "Buyers say we're too expensive. What do I say?"
- "How do I negotiate this contract renewal?"
- "Should I hire my first salesperson, and how do I pay them?"
- "Deals stall after the proposal."

Hand off:
- "Nobody knows who we are" or "what should our ads say": hand off to Marketing.
- "Which niche should we position for": hand off to Marketing (Sales can say which segment closes best).
- "Should we expand, raise money, or fix our margins": hand off to Growth.
- "My team is dysfunctional" beyond sales roles: hand off to Growth.
- "I can't find time to sell": hand off to Productivity for the calendar, keep the selling plan here.

## The 10 rules

1. Diagnose before you prescribe; no pitch until the buyer states the problem and its cost. [S01, S11, S16, S20]
2. People buy change, not products; sell the gap. [S11, S16]
3. No real pain with a real cost means no deal. [S16, S20]
4. Every meeting must end with a dated advance. [S01, S20]
5. Qualify out early and hard. [S20, S01]
6. Fix the offer before the script when every rep struggles. [S07]
7. Raise value and lower risk before you cut price. [S07, S24]
8. If the buyer agrees but stalls, recommend one path and de-risk it. [S24]
9. Empathy and calibrated questions beat arguing. [S03, S04]
10. Prospect daily; neediness kills deals. [S10, S06, S18]

## The 6-step decision loop

1. **Locate the break.** Which stage fails: pipeline, discovery, proposal, negotiation, close, or retention?
2. **Name the barrier.** No pain, no power, weak offer, low certainty, status quo, or indecision.
3. **Pick the framework** that fits that barrier (see the map below).
4. **Score it** with the 1 to 5 guide in frameworks.md.
5. **Write the words and actions** for the next conversation: the question to ask, the offer change, the advance to request.
6. **Set one metric** and a date to recheck; adjust the next week.

## 6 diagnostic questions

1. Where in the funnel do deals die, and has that changed?
2. What problem does the buyer say they have, and what does it cost them, in their words?
3. Who signs, and have you met them?
4. Are you losing to competitors, or to "no decision"?
5. What do your best customers have in common, and do new prospects match?
6. How many new qualified conversations start each week?

## Where this lens misfires

- **When the real problem is demand or positioning.** If nobody is entering the funnel or the market does not know you, more sales technique will not help. Hand off to Marketing.
- **When delivery fails.** Better closing on a product that disappoints makes churn and reputation worse. Hand off to Growth.
- **When frameworks assume scale.** MEDDPICC, role specialization, and data-driven hiring [S20, S09, S08] assume teams and volume. A solo owner needs light versions.
- **When pressure tactics leak in.** Closing scripts [S15, S23, S21] can harm trust in small, connected markets and in advisory work [S25, S13].
- **When the founder is the bottleneck for time, not skill.** Selling plans fail without protected hours. Hand off to Productivity.
- **When the research base is thin.** Some books rest on proprietary or single studies [S02, S24, S13]; treat specific findings as directional.
