# Principles: Sales department

Built from the 25 Sales books (S01 to S25): decision rules with the books that back them, then the places where the books disagree.

Confidence notes: "strong" means five or more books agree, "moderate" means three or four, "single source" means one or two books.

## Decision rules

1. Diagnose before you prescribe: no pitch until the buyer has stated the problem and its cost. [S01, S11, S16, S20, S25] Strong, five books.
2. People buy a change in their situation, not your product, so sell the gap between now and better. [S11, S16, S07] Moderate, three books.
3. The buyer should say the value out loud; value you assert is weaker than value they state. [S01, S16, S04] Moderate, three books.
4. If there is no real pain with a real cost, there is no deal, however friendly the prospect. [S16, S20, S11, S01] Moderate, four books.
5. Judge every meeting by the specific next commitment it produced, not by how well it went. [S01, S20, S18] Moderate, three books.
6. Qualify out early: time spent on deals you cannot win is stolen from deals you can. [S20, S01, S07] Moderate, three books.
7. Find and test a champion with influence; a friendly contact without power is not a champion. [S20, S16] Single source, two books.
8. Know who signs, how they decide, and what paperwork stands between yes and payment. [S20, S16] Single source, two books.
9. A weak offer cannot be fixed by better scripts; fix the offer first when every rep struggles. [S07] Single source, one book; consistent with [S11].
10. Raise perceived value before you cut price: improve outcome, likelihood, speed, and ease. [S07, S11] Single source, two books.
11. Lower the buyer's risk to make deciding easy: guarantees, pilots, phased starts. [S07, S24, S05] Moderate, three books.
12. When a buyer agrees on the problem but stalls, recommend one path and narrow the options. [S24, S19] Single source, two books.
13. Do not answer indecision with more information; more options deepen paralysis. [S24] Single source, one book.
14. Empathy before argument: show you understand the other side's view before trying to change it. [S03, S04, S25, S13] Moderate, four books.
15. Never argue with a buyer; agree with the feeling, then redirect. [S04, S21, S03, S15] Moderate, four books.
16. Ask open "how" and "what" questions to make the other side work on your problem. [S03, S01, S11] Moderate, three books.
17. Expect objections and prepare responses for the top five before they happen. [S22, S23, S15, S21] Moderate, four books.
18. Find the real concern under the stated objection before answering it; price is often a cover. [S21, S22, S03, S15] Moderate, four books.
19. Most objections reflect low certainty or skipped discovery; go back and build those. [S15, S22, S11] Moderate, three books.
20. An empty pipeline causes most sales failures; prospect every day, not when desperate. [S10, S18, S09, S21] Moderate, four books.
21. Neediness repels buyers; a full pipeline lets you sell calmly and walk away. [S10, S06, S07] Moderate, three books.
22. Pick a short list of target accounts and pursue them on purpose rather than waiting for leads. [S18, S09] Single source, two books.
23. Lead with the buyer's issues and outcomes, not with your company's history or features. [S18, S02, S13, S14] Moderate, four books.
24. Teach buyers something new about their business that leads back to your unique strength. [S02, S13] Single source, two books.
25. Keep respectful tension with the status quo; too much comfort leads to no decision. [S02, S11, S16] Moderate, three books.
26. Trust grows with credibility, reliability, and closeness, and shrinks with self-interest. [S25, S04, S13, S14] Moderate, four books.
27. Honesty is a sales strategy because buyers can now check everything you say. [S13, S25, S23] Moderate, three books.
28. Use persuasion principles only truthfully; fake scarcity or fake proof costs more than it wins. [S05, S13, S25] Moderate, three books.
29. Belief in what you sell shows in tone and conviction; if you do not believe, fix the product or switch. [S15, S21, S23, S12] Moderate, four books.
30. Rejection is about the offer and timing, not about you; separate the two to keep activity up. [S12, S13, S22, S17] Moderate, four books.
31. Persistence wins more follow-ups than talent, but stop when it becomes pressure. [S21, S10, S17, S13] Moderate, four books.
32. Protect prime selling hours for selling and move admin elsewhere. [S10, S18] Single source, two books.
33. Specialize sales roles once volume allows; closers who must also prospect stop prospecting. [S09, S08] Single source, two books.
34. Hire salespeople for coachability and curiosity, and test them in the interview. [S08, S20] Single source, two books.
35. Coach one skill at a time, chosen from funnel data. [S08, S20] Single source, two books.
36. Pay reps for customers who stay, not only for customers who sign. [S08] Single source, one book.
37. In big deals, heavy closing pressure lowers win rates; in small one-call deals, a clear ask helps. [S01, S23, S15] Moderate, three books.
38. Control the frame of the meeting: agenda, time, and who is qualifying whom. [S06, S02, S15] Moderate, three books.

## Where the books disagree

**1. Close hard or don't close: Ziglar and Belfort versus Rackham.** [S23, S15] teach many closing techniques and steady pushing to a yes. [S01] found that closing techniques hurt in large sales. *When each is right:* one-call, lower-priced consumer sales reward a clear, confident ask [S23, S15]. Multi-meeting B2B deals reward earned advances, not closes [S01].

**2. Relationships versus challenge: Carnegie and Gitomer versus Dixon and Adamson.** [S04, S14, S25] say liking and trust drive sales. [S02] argues that relationship builders underperform in complex sales and that teaching and tension win. *When each is right:* local, repeat, and referral businesses run on relationships. Commoditized, multi-stakeholder B2B rewards insight. Most firms need both: trust earns the right to challenge [S25].

**3. Change the buyer's mind versus help them decide: Challenger versus JOLT.** [S02, S11] focus on making the status quo feel costly. [S24], from one of the same authors, argues many stalls are fear of a wrong choice, where more pressure on the problem makes it worse. *When each is right:* if the buyer doubts the problem, build the case for change. If they agree and still delay, recommend and de-risk.

**4. Persistence versus respect: Cardone and Blount versus Pink and Maister.** [S21, S10] push persistence and refusing to accept the first no. [S13, S25] stress attunement and low self-orientation. *When each is right:* early prospecting brush-offs are reflexes and deserve a second try [S22]. Once a qualified buyer gives a reasoned no, more pushing burns trust, which hurts most in small, connected markets.

**5. Power and status versus empathy: Klaff versus Voss and Carnegie.** [S06] says hold the dominant frame and never seem needy. [S03, S04] say make the other side feel understood and important. *When each is right:* with investors and powerful buyers who test you, status matters [S06]. In tense talks or with nervous buyers, empathy unlocks information [S03]. Voss also warns against appearing weak, so the two meet at calm confidence.

**6. Specialize roles versus everyone sells: Ross and Roberge versus Weinberg and Cardone.** [S09, S08] split prospecting from closing. [S18, S21] expect every seller to prospect. *When each is right:* specialize when you have enough reps and steady demand. Under about four reps, or with a founder selling, one person must do both, and time blocking matters more than structure.

**7. Script versus authenticity: Belfort and Jones versus Pink and Maister.** [S15, S19, S22] provide wording and patterns to practice. [S13, S25] warn that canned behavior erodes trust. *When each is right:* scripts help new reps and repeatable openers. Experienced sellers in advisory sales should adapt words to the person, keeping structure but not text.

**8. Price up versus negotiate down: Hormozi versus Voss.** [S07] says raise price and build value so negotiation barely happens. [S03] gives tools for tough price bargaining. *When each is right:* when you set the offer, build value and hold price [S07]. When you face a buyer with leverage or are the buyer yourself, negotiate skillfully [S03].

**9. Mindset first versus process first: Tracy and Mandino versus Roberge and McMahon.** [S12, S17] place belief and habit at the center. [S08, S20] place data, process, and qualification at the center. *When each is right:* if activity is low because of fear, start with mindset. If activity is high but results are poor, fix process and qualification.
