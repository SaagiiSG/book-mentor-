# Playbooks: Marketing department

Built from the 25 Marketing books (M01 to M19, M21, M24, M26 to M29) and the frameworks in frameworks.md. Each playbook: diagnose first, then act.

Quote policy: verbatim quotes appear ONLY from the author talks and public-domain texts listed in talks.md, always with a T id. Text of copyrighted books is never quoted. Everything else is paraphrase cited by book id.

## 1. "I don't get enough leads"
**Ask first**
1. How many leads did you get last month, and from which sources?
2. What does a customer pay you over a year, and what can you afford to spend to get one?
3. Which of warm outreach, content, cold outreach, and ads are you doing, and how often?
4. When a lead arrives, what happens next?
**Plan**
1. List lead sources against the Core Four [M01]. Find the one already working best.
2. Apply More, Better, New [M01]: raise volume on that source first.
3. Set a fixed daily lead-action number (Rule of 100, scaled to your size) [M01].
4. Add one lead magnet that solves a narrow problem for your best buyer [M01, M10].
5. Add a referral ask at the moment of delivered value [M01, M19].
6. Track source to sale for every lead [M14].
**Metric** Engaged leads per week by source, and cost per engaged lead.
**Common mistake** Adding a new channel before running the current one at full volume.

## 2. "My ads aren't working"
**Ask first**
1. What is the cost per lead and cost per customer, versus what a customer is worth?
2. Who is the ad shown to, and how aware are they of the problem?
3. What does the landing page say in its first line?
4. Is the offer one people want, or is the ad doing all the work?
**Plan**
1. Check tracking first: can you see which ad produced which sale [M14]?
2. Match the hook to awareness level: cold audiences get the problem, warm get proof or the offer [M12].
3. Rewrite headlines around a specific benefit for a named reader [M13, M14].
4. Check the landing page with the SB7 clarity test [M02].
5. Test three hooks at a time; keep the offer constant [M24, M10].
6. If no hook wins, fix the offer, not the ad. Hand off to Sales [S07].
**Metric** Cost per acquired customer against first-year customer value.
**Common mistake** Changing audience, creative, and offer at once, so you learn nothing.

## 3. "We sound like everyone else"
**Ask first**
1. What would your best customers do if you did not exist?
2. What do you have that those alternatives lack?
3. Which customers love you most, and what do they have in common?
4. How do prospects describe you today?
**Plan**
1. Interview five to ten best customers [M18].
2. Work through the Positioning Components: alternatives, attributes, value, best fit, category [M18].
3. Choose a category strategy: head to head, a subsegment you can own, or a new category [M18, M05].
4. Pick one word or idea to own [M06].
5. Draw a two-axis map to confirm the open spot [M07].
6. Rewrite the homepage headline and one-liner from the result [M02].
7. Decide what you will stop saying or selling [M06].
**Metric** Share of sales calls where the prospect repeats your positioning back, and close rate on best-fit leads.
**Common mistake** Picking a difference buyers do not care about.

## 4. "My website gets traffic but no inquiries"
**Ask first**
1. Can a stranger say what you offer within seconds of landing?
2. Is there one obvious next step?
3. Where does the traffic come from, and how aware are those visitors?
4. Do you show price, proof, and how it works?
**Plan**
1. Run the grunt test on the homepage [M02].
2. Put one direct CTA and one transitional CTA above the fold [M02].
3. Lead with the customer's problem and result, not your history [M02, M12].
4. Add specific proof: numbers, named results, reviews [M14, M04].
5. Answer price and common objections on the page [M17].
6. Add a lead magnet for visitors not ready to talk [M01].
**Metric** Visitor to inquiry rate on key pages.
**Common mistake** Redesigning visuals while the words stay vague.

## 5. "Which channel should I use?"
**Ask first**
1. Where do your best customers already spend attention?
2. What is your budget in money and in hours per week?
3. How long can you wait for results?
4. Which channels have you tried, and with what numbers?
**Plan**
1. List candidate channels across the Core Four [M01].
2. Rank them by how well they reach your best buyer and what they cost.
3. Run discovery tests on the top two or three, with small budgets and a fixed end date [M24].
4. Track cost per customer for each [M14].
5. Focus on the winner and apply More, Better, New [M01]; drop the rest.
6. Revisit when that channel plateaus; channels age fast [M24].
**Metric** Cost per customer and volume available per channel.
**Common mistake** Choosing a channel because a competitor uses it.

## 6. "Nobody refers us"
**Ask first**
1. Do you ask for referrals, and when?
2. Is there anything about the experience people would mention?
3. Is your customer's use of you visible to others?
4. Do customers have a simple way to pass you on?
**Plan**
1. Score the experience on STEPPS [M03].
2. Add one remarkable element to the product or service [M08].
3. Make it public: something visible or shareable [M03].
4. Ask at the moment of peak satisfaction, with a ready-made message [M01, M19].
5. Give referrers social currency (insider status, recognition) before cash [M03].
**Metric** Share of new customers who came by referral.
**Common mistake** Paying cash bounties for an experience nobody finds worth talking about.

## 7. "I don't know what to post"
**Ask first**
1. What questions do prospects ask before buying?
2. What do you know that your buyers need?
3. Which one platform do your buyers use most?
4. How many hours a week can you give this?
**Plan**
1. List every question from sales calls and emails [M17].
2. Cover the Big 5 first: price, problems, comparisons, reviews, best-of [M17].
3. Show the process: what you are working on, learning, and getting wrong [M27].
4. Pick one platform and a fixed schedule [M01].
5. Frame niche knowledge under a broad theme strangers care about, and write the first three seconds first [M29].
6. Make posts native to that platform; mostly value, with periodic asks [M16, M28].
7. Push readers to an email list [M10].
**Metric** Leads or subscribers from content per month.
**Common mistake** Posting on five platforms irregularly, then quitting at month two.

## 8. "We're a small player against a big competitor"
**Ask first**
1. What does the leader own in buyers' minds?
2. Who is badly served by the leader?
3. Can you be first in a narrower category?
4. What can you do that the leader cannot copy fast?
**Plan**
1. Map the product ladder in your category [M05].
2. Position against the leader's strength, turning it into a weakness for some buyers (Law of the Opposite) [M06].
3. Pick a subsegment where you can be the big fish [M18].
4. Narrow the message to one word you can own [M06].
5. Build one remarkable feature the leader would not bother with [M08].
**Metric** Win rate in the chosen subsegment.
**Common mistake** Copying the leader's broad message with a smaller budget.

## 9. "I want to launch a new product"
**Ask first**
1. Who is it for, and who is it not for?
2. Is it new to buyers or an improvement on what they use?
3. Who will talk about it first?
4. How will you know in 30 days whether it works?
**Plan**
1. Define best fit and alternatives [M18].
2. Frame it as a new opportunity if buyers have tried other options [M11].
3. Design for STEPPS, especially social currency and triggers [M03].
4. Seed it with your sneezers and existing list first [M08, M01].
5. Run a small launch to measure response before spending [M14, M24].
6. Check line extension risk to your main brand [M05, M06].
**Metric** Conversion of existing list and repeat or referral rate in the first month.
**Common mistake** Launching to everyone at once with no measure of what worked.

## 10. "My emails get ignored"
**Ask first**
1. How did people join the list, and what did they expect?
2. What do subject lines promise?
3. How often do you send, and what share is promotion?
4. Is there one clear reader in mind?
**Plan**
1. Write subject lines like headlines: specific benefit, curiosity gap [M13, M04].
2. Add a welcome sequence that tells your story and opens loops [M10].
3. Write to one reader around one simple, concrete idea [M04].
4. Mix mostly value with clear periodic offers [M16].
5. Test subject lines and track clicks to sales [M14].
**Metric** Click rate and revenue per email sent.
**Common mistake** Sending only offers and wondering why opens fall.

## 11. "We're competing on price"
**Ask first**
1. Why do customers who pay more choose you?
2. What is the buyer's real alternative?
3. Do your materials show value or only features?
4. Does anything signal quality?
**Plan**
1. Rebuild positioning around value for the best-fit buyer [M18].
2. Narrow to a smallest viable market that values what you do [M07].
3. Use costly signals and reframing to raise perceived value [M21].
4. Show specific proof of results [M14].
5. Hand off price structure and offer stack to Sales [S07].
**Metric** Average selling price and win rate on best-fit leads.
**Common mistake** Discounting harder instead of changing who you sell to.

## 12. "Should I rebrand?"
**Ask first**
1. What problem will the rebrand solve?
2. Do customers recognize your current assets?
3. Is the issue the look, the name, or the positioning?
4. What will you lose?
**Plan**
1. Audit distinctive assets: which ones are recognized [M15]?
2. If the problem is positioning, fix positioning, not the logo [M18].
3. Keep recognized assets; change only what hurts [M15, M13].
4. If renaming, check that the name hints at the benefit [M05].
5. Roll out consistently across every touchpoint [M15].
**Metric** Recognition and inquiry volume before and after.
**Common mistake** Rebranding because the owner is bored.

## 13. "We get leads but they don't convert"
**Ask first**
1. Are the leads your best-fit customer?
2. What happens between first contact and sale?
3. How fast do you respond?
4. What do lost leads say?
**Plan**
1. Check targeting and lead magnet: are they attracting the right person [M01, M18]?
2. Build lead nurture for not-yet-ready leads [M19].
3. Send assignment content before calls [M17].
4. Address the three false beliefs in nurture content [M11].
5. Hand off call skills and follow-up to Sales [S01, S11].
**Metric** Lead to customer rate by source.
**Common mistake** Blaming sales when marketing attracts the wrong leads.

## 14. "Growth has stalled"
**Ask first**
1. Is the stall in new customers, repeat, or price?
2. Would customers be very disappointed without you?
3. Has your main channel saturated?
4. Are you reaching new buyers or the same ones?
**Plan**
1. Run the must-have survey [M24].
2. If below the bar, fix product first; hand off to Growth [G05].
3. Check penetration: are you reaching light and new buyers [M15]?
4. Run discovery tests for a second channel [M24, M01].
5. Set up a weekly test cycle with ICE [M24].
**Metric** New customers per month and North Star Metric [M24].
**Common mistake** Spending more on a saturated channel.

## 15. "My app or subscription has poor retention"
**Ask first**
1. When do users drop off?
2. What triggers people to come back?
3. What does the user invest that makes it better?
4. Is the core value delivered early?
**Plan**
1. Map the Hook Model for your product [M09].
2. Find the aha moment and shorten the path to it [M24].
3. Add a useful external trigger tied to the user's routine [M09].
4. Build investment: saved data, preferences, content [M09].
5. Check the Manipulation Matrix before shipping [M09].
**Metric** Cohort retention at week 1 and week 4.
**Common mistake** Adding notifications without improving the reward.

## 16. "Should we start a podcast, YouTube, or blog?"
**Ask first**
1. Where do your buyers learn?
2. What is your content tilt?
3. Can you commit for 12 months?
4. How will it make money?
**Plan**
1. Pick the format that fits the owner's strengths: talking, writing, or showing [M28].
2. Choose one platform buyers use [M01].
3. Plan topics from buyer questions [M17] and from the work in progress [M27].
4. Test hooks on the first seconds of each episode or video [M29].
5. Publish on a fixed schedule; drive subscribers to email [M10].
6. Review leads at 90 days; decide to continue or switch [M24].
**Metric** Owned subscribers and leads from content.
**Common mistake** Starting all three, then stopping all three.

## 17. "Nobody knows who I am" (building a founder brand)
**Ask first**
1. Why does the business exist, beyond making money?
2. What are you good at on camera, in writing, or in person?
3. What could you show every day from your actual work?
4. How many hours a week can you give this for a year?
**Plan**
1. Write the why, how, and what in three lines; check it against recent decisions [M26].
2. Choose one format and platform that fit your strengths [M28].
3. Share one small piece of process or a lesson each day [M27].
4. Write several hooks per post and keep the winners [M29].
5. Give mostly value, with a clear ask once a week [M16].
6. Track which posts bring inquiries, not just views [M14].
**Metric** Inquiries per month that mention your content.
**Common mistake** Chasing views from strangers who will never buy.
