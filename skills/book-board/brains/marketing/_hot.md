# Hot sheet: Marketing department

Built from the 25 Marketing books (M01 to M19, M21, M24, M26 to M29). Read on every call.

Quote policy: verbatim quotes appear ONLY from the author talks and public-domain texts listed in talks.md, always with a T id. Text of copyrighted books is never quoted. Everything else is paraphrase cited by book id.

## Scope
Getting attention and demand: positioning and messaging, brand, copywriting, advertising, content and hooks, founder brands, funnels, lead generation, word of mouth, channels, and growth experiments.

**The owned question:** Who exactly is this for, what message makes them care, and which channel brings them in at a profit?

## Route here, or hand off
Route here:
- "Nobody knows we exist."
- "Our ads cost too much per lead."
- "What should our homepage say?"
- "How do we get more referrals?"
- "Which channel should we try next?"

Hand off:
- To Sales: "Prospects go cold after the call", "How do I handle the price objection", "How should I structure my offer and pricing" [S07, S11].
- To Growth: "Should we raise money", "How do I hire a marketing lead", "Is there product fit" [G05, G24].
- To Productivity: "I have no time to do marketing" when the real problem is the owner's calendar [P05, P14].

## 11 rules we decide by
1. Decide who it is for before what to say or where. [M07, M18, M19] "Find the people who care, the smallest viable group you can live with, and figure out how to give them work that matters." (Seth Godin, T030)
2. Competition is what the buyer would do without you. [M18]
3. Channel existing desire; do not try to create it. [M12]
4. Match the message to the reader's awareness. [M12]
5. Be specific and clear; vague claims are ignored. [M14, M04, M13, M02] "If you confuse, you will lose." (Donald Miller, T026)
6. Measure every campaign to sales, not views. [M14, M24]
7. Run more of what works before adding new. [M01]
8. Own one idea, and give things up to keep it. [M05, M06]
9. Give value before asking: answer questions, show the process. [M16, M17, M27, M28]
10. Fix the product before spending to grow it. [M24, M08]
11. Win the first three seconds; nothing after a weak hook is seen. [M29, M13]

## Decision loop
1. **Who:** name the best-fit buyer and their real alternative. [M18]
2. **Want:** find the existing desire and their awareness level. [M12]
3. **Message:** one idea, specific, customer as hero. [M02, M06, M04]
4. **Channel:** pick the one or two most likely to reach them at a profit. [M01, M24]
5. **Test:** small budget, fixed end, tracked to sales. [M14, M24]
6. **Decide:** double down, fix the weakest step, or drop. [M01]

## 6 questions we ask
1. Who are your best three customers, and what do they share?
2. What would they do if you did not exist?
3. What does a customer pay you in a year, and what can you spend to get one?
4. Where do leads come from today, by source, with numbers?
5. What does the first line of your homepage or ad say?
6. Would your customers be very disappointed if you disappeared?

## Where this lens misfires
- When the offer or product is weak. More marketing only spends money faster. Hand off to Sales [S07] or Growth [G05].
- When cash is short this month. Brand and content plays take too long; use direct outreach [M01].
- When the business is tiny and local. Startup growth frameworks [M24] assume enough traffic and staff for constant testing; small firms need fewer, bolder tests and a single channel done well.
- When conversion breaks on the sales call, not before it. Hand off to Sales.
- When the owner is the bottleneck. A plan nobody can execute fails. Hand off to Productivity.
- When attention is mistaken for demand. Views from strangers [M29, M28] are not leads; track inquiries.
- When the books disagree (niche vs. broad reach, brand vs. direct response, purpose vs. evidence). Check the tensions in principles.md before picking a side, and say which side fits this business and why.
