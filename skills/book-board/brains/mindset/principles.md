# Principles: Mindset department

Decision rules built from the 15 Mindset books (M01 to M15), paraphrased, each with its sources and a confidence level based on how many of our books clearly support it and how well the idea holds up outside them. Quote policy: verbatim quotes only from author talks and the public-domain Meditations text (T102), with a T id; copyrighted book text is never quoted.

## Rules

1. Separate what happened from the story you tell about it before deciding anything. [M02, M10, M13, M14] Confidence: high, four books agree.
2. Spend energy on what you control: your effort, choices, standards and responses. Release the rest. [M02, M10, M05, M13] Confidence: high, four books agree.
3. A setback may not be your fault, but your response is your responsibility. [M13, M01, M03] Confidence: high, three books agree.
4. Treat obstacles as material: ask what this setback makes possible or teaches. [M02, M10, M11] Confidence: medium to high, three books agree; survivor bias in the stories.
5. If the cause of suffering can be removed, remove it; accept and reframe only what cannot be changed. [M03, M10] Confidence: high; Frankl is explicit.
6. Abilities can be developed; treat a gap as "not yet" and pick a strategy, not a verdict. [M09, M12] Confidence: medium; the idea is sound, measured effects of brief interventions are small.
7. Notice your fixed-mindset triggers (challenge, setback, criticism, someone better) before trying to change them. [M09] Confidence: medium, one book, consistent with M11 on ego.
8. Praise and judge process (strategy, persistence, learning), for others and yourself, not talent or results alone. [M09, M12, M15] Confidence: medium, three books agree.
9. Action comes before motivation; when stuck, start the smallest piece now. [M13, M15, M01] Confidence: medium to high, three books agree.
10. Show up on a fixed schedule for important work, whatever your mood. [M15, M12, M01] Confidence: medium to high, three books agree.
11. The task you avoid most is often the one that matters most; check the plan, then do it first. [M15, M13] Confidence: medium; fear can also be a valid warning.
12. Every goal brings its own pain; choose goals whose struggle you can accept. [M13, M12, M15] Confidence: medium, three books agree.
13. Hold one stable top-level goal; change tactics freely when they fail. [M12, M04] Confidence: medium, two books agree.
14. Improve with deliberate practice: a precise stretch, full focus, fast feedback, reflection. [M12] Confidence: medium to high; well supported beyond this book.
15. Write your purpose, what you will give for it, and a plan; review it often. [M04, M12] Confidence: medium; drop Hill's belief-attracts-riches claims.
16. Know what the work is for and who it serves; meaning outlasts pressure. [M03, M12, M13] Confidence: medium to high, three books agree.
17. Do not aim at happiness directly; it follows from good work, people and values. [M03, M08, M13] Confidence: medium, three books agree, from different angles.
18. Measure yourself by values that are real, help others and are in your control, not status. [M13, M11, M07] Confidence: medium, three books agree.
19. Confidence is earned from the work; ego is a story you tell yourself. Stay a student. [M11, M09] Confidence: medium, two books agree.
20. After a win, look for the luck and the mistakes inside it before you plan the next round. [M11, M07] Confidence: medium to high, two books, consistent with research on luck and hindsight.
21. Decide what enough is before you chase more, and stop the goalposts moving. [M07, M08, M11] Confidence: medium to high, three books agree.
22. Do not spend to impress; people notice you far less than you think. [M07, M13, M06] Confidence: medium, three books agree.
23. Play long games with long-term people; avoid anything that could knock you out of the game. [M08, M07, M12] Confidence: high, three books agree.
24. Self-sabotage protects an unmet need; find the need instead of blaming willpower. [M14, M13] Confidence: low to medium; plausible, little direct research.
25. Read strong feelings as information (anger at a boundary, envy at a want), then act on the message, not the feeling. [M14, M05] Confidence: low to medium, two books.
26. Stop rescuing and controlling others; let them, then decide what you will do. Keep standards and boundaries. [M05, M02] Confidence: medium, two books agree.
27. Tolerate looking foolish; growth needs being bad at something first. [M13, M09, M15] Confidence: medium, three books agree.
28. Use hard, chosen effort for a defined push, with an end date and health guardrails. [M01, M12, M03] Confidence: medium; intensity without limits risks burnout.
29. Expect setbacks and difficult people ahead of time so they do not knock you over. [M02, M10] Confidence: medium, two books agree.
30. Get honest peers around you who will challenge your story. [M04, M11, M08] Confidence: medium, three books agree.
31. Examine the money beliefs you grew up with; they shape pricing, spending and risk. [M06, M14, M07] Confidence: medium, three books agree.
32. When the issue is exhaustion, constant dread, numbness or hopelessness, stop pushing and hand off to Psychology and Mental Health. [M03, M14, M01 limits] Confidence: high; a safety rule, not a book claim.

## Where the books disagree

**1. Push through versus ease off: Can't Hurt Me [M01] and The War of Art [M15] versus The Mountain Is You [M14] and Man's Search for Meaning [M03].** M01 and M15 say discomfort and dread are things to override: show up and push. M14 says a stuck pattern is protecting a need, and forcing it can deepen the conflict; M03 says suffering is never required when its cause can be removed. Push when the plan is sound, the owner is healthy, and the block is plain avoidance. Ease off and look underneath when force has failed repeatedly, the same pattern returns, or there are signs of exhaustion or low mood; then hand off to Psychology and Mental Health.

**2. Burning desire versus wanting less: Think and Grow Rich [M04] versus The Almanack of Naval Ravikant [M08] and The Psychology of Money [M07].** M04 wants an intense, constant desire for a definite goal. M08 treats desire as the source of unhappiness, and M07 warns that rising wants keep the goalposts moving. Desire is right for a defined, chosen goal in the building phase. Wanting less is right once basic security is reached, or when the owner is never satisfied whatever they achieve.

**3. Belief shapes results versus results are partly luck: Think and Grow Rich [M04] versus The Psychology of Money [M07] and Ego Is the Enemy [M11].** M04 says a firmly held belief drives achievement. M07 and M11 say luck and risk are large and success stories are rewritten after the fact. Use belief for motivation and persistence; use the luck view when judging past results, planning risk, and judging other people's failures.

**4. Passion versus purpose: Grit [M12] versus Ego Is the Enemy [M11].** M12 treats passion (sustained interest) as half of grit and something to foster. M11 warns that passion can be a form of ego and prefers sober purpose. Passion helps choose and stay in a field over years. Purpose is safer for deciding what to do this week, especially when excitement is driving rash bets.

**5. Reframe the obstacle versus remove it: The Obstacle Is the Way [M10] and Meditations [M02] versus Man's Search for Meaning [M03].** M10 and M02 lean toward accepting and using what happens. M03 insists that if the cause of suffering can be removed, you should remove it. Reframe when the event is past or truly outside your control. Remove it when you can: leave the bad partnership, fire the toxic client, close the losing line.

**6. Care less about others' opinions versus build on others: The Subtle Art [M13] and The Let Them Theory [M05] versus Think and Grow Rich [M04] and Grit [M12].** M13 and M05 say stop caring what people think and stop managing them. M04 builds on a mastermind and M12 on cultures and mentors that hold you to a standard. Ignore the opinions of people who do not matter to your values or the work. Seek out and listen hard to a few chosen people who see your work clearly.

**7. Debt and boldness versus room for error: Rich Dad Poor Dad [M06] versus The Psychology of Money [M07].** M06 sees fear as what keeps people poor and treats debt as a tool to buy assets. M07 puts survival first: avoid ruin, keep a buffer. Boldness suits an owner whose fear blocks sensible, small, cash-flowing bets. Room for error suits most owner-funded firms and any move that could sink the business. Money decisions themselves go to Business.

**8. Fixed self-acceptance versus endless growth: Mindset [M09] versus The Mountain Is You [M14] and The Subtle Art [M13].** M09 pushes toward developing abilities and choosing challenges. M13 and M14 also stress accepting limits and who you are now. Dweck herself says you need not grow in everything and choose where to focus. Use growth for skills that matter to the business; use acceptance for traits and limits that are not worth the fight.

**9. Self-discipline alone versus systems and environment: Can't Hurt Me [M01] and The War of Art [M15] versus the Productivity department.** M01 and M15 put the weight on inner will. Most habit research puts it on design: make the action easy, cue it, track it. Use will to decide and to restart; use systems so you do not need will daily. Systems questions go to Productivity.
