# Playbooks: Mindset department

Step-by-step plans for situations owners and founders bring to this department, built from the 15 Mindset books (M01 to M15) and the frameworks in frameworks.md. All paraphrased; verbatim quotes only from author talks and the public-domain Meditations text (T102), with a T id.

## 1. I keep putting off the one project that matters

**Ask first**
- What is the project, and how long has it been on your list?
- What do you do instead when you sit down to it?
- What would happen if it went badly? If it went well?
- Is the plan sound, or is part of you doubting the project itself?

**Plan**
1. Name the project and rate avoidance and impact (Resistance Compass, M15).
2. Check the plan with Business if the doubt is about the idea, not the work.
3. Shrink the first step to something you can start in five minutes (Do Something Principle, M13).
4. Set a fixed session, at least three times a week, and judge it only by showing up (Turning Pro, M15).
5. Write the story you tell when you avoid it, and test it (Story Check, M02, M10).

**Sign to watch:** sessions kept per week on the avoided project.
**Common mistake:** waiting to feel ready, or spending the week on easier work that looks productive.

## 2. A setback has knocked me flat (lost client, failed launch, bad review)

**Ask first**
- What exactly happened, in facts only?
- What are you telling yourself it means?
- What can still be changed, and what cannot?
- What did this setback free up or reveal?

**Plan**
1. Write the event and the story separately (Story Check, M02).
2. Sort what you control from what you do not (Control Sort, M02, M10).
3. Fix what can be fixed first; reframe only what cannot (Choose Your Stance, M03).
4. Work it through Perception, Action, Will: one next step today, then the next (M10).
5. Separate fault from responsibility and take one responsible action (M13).

**Sign to watch:** days since the setback that you took a forward action.
**Common mistake:** reframing too early, before the fixable part is fixed, or replaying the loss without a next task.

## 3. I feel like a fraud

**Ask first**
- In which rooms does the feeling show up?
- What evidence do you have that you can do this work?
- Are you comparing yourself to people at a much later stage?
- Is the feeling stopping you from acting, or just uncomfortable?

**Plan**
1. Write the Cookie Jar: ten things you have done that were hard (M01).
2. Place confidence between arrogance and self-doubt: pride in the work you did, not in titles (M11).
3. Replace "I'm a fraud" with a believable rung ("I have delivered for many clients and I'm still learning") (Believable Story Ladder, M14).
4. Name the skill gap that is real and treat it as not yet, with a practice plan (M09, M12).
5. Ask one trusted peer what they see (Mastermind, M04).

**Sign to watch:** big asks or rooms you avoided last month that you entered this month.
**Common mistake:** trying to fix the feeling with more credentials instead of more reps.

## 4. I can't charge what I'm worth or ask for money

**Ask first**
- What did you learn about money growing up?
- What do you fear will happen if you raise prices or chase payment?
- What are your current prices compared to the value delivered?
- Who would you have to become to ask without flinching?

**Plan**
1. Run the Money Story Audit and name the belief behind the underpricing (M14, M06).
2. Map the self-sabotage: what does undercharging protect you from (M14)?
3. Write the believable replacement belief (money as a tool that funds better service) (M14).
4. Use Premeditation of Adversity: expect some pushback, write your reply, decide what loss you can absorb (M10).
5. Raise prices for new clients first; take the decision on numbers to Business.

**Sign to watch:** price asked versus price you used to ask, and how many clients actually leave.
**Common mistake:** treating it as a pricing strategy problem only, when the block is a belief.

## 5. I'm afraid to be visible (post, sell, speak, ask)

**Ask first**
- What exactly do you avoid?
- Whose judgment do you fear?
- What has actually happened the times you did it?

**Plan**
1. List the avoided acts from mildest to hardest (Embarrassment Reps, M13).
2. Do the mildest this week and record what really happened (M13).
3. Log fixed-mindset triggers when you hesitate (M09).
4. Decide whose opinions matter (customers, a few peers) and let the rest go (Let Them, M05; Values Audit, M13).
5. Move up one rung per week.

**Sign to watch:** visible acts per week.
**Common mistake:** waiting for confidence first; confidence comes from the reps.
**Hand-off:** what to post or say goes to Business; this is only the fear.

## 6. I keep quitting or switching ideas

**Ask first**
- How many ideas or ventures in the last two years?
- At what point did each stop: plateau, boredom, first failure?
- What would the one idea you stuck with look like in five years?

**Plan**
1. Write a top-level goal and link current tasks to it (Goal Hierarchy, M12).
2. Separate tactics that failed from the goal; swap the tactic, keep the goal (M12).
3. Choose the hard thing and set a natural stopping point before you start (Hard Thing Rule, M12).
4. Check the struggle: is the pain of this path one you can accept (Choose Your Struggle, M13)?
5. Apply the ten-year test to the idea and partners (Long-Term Games, M08).

**Sign to watch:** weeks on the same goal without switching.
**Common mistake:** calling stubbornness on a bad goal grit. Quitting a low-level tactic is smart.

## 7. I've lost my drive, and I don't know why I'm doing this

**Ask first**
- When did the drive go? What changed?
- Who does the work serve, and do you still care about them?
- Are you sleeping, and do you feel numb or hopeless most days?

**Plan**
1. Screen first: if numbness, hopelessness, poor sleep or constant exhaustion are present, hand off to Psychology and Mental Health.
2. Run the Meaning Check: who it serves, a one-line why (M03).
3. Check whether you are under-demanded: is the work too easy or too repetitive (M03)?
4. Run a Values Audit: are you measuring yourself by status (M13)?
5. Reconnect one task a week to the people served; cut one thing that serves none of it.

**Sign to watch:** whether you can say the why in one line, and whether one task a week feels worth doing.
**Common mistake:** answering lost meaning with more pressure (M01 used in the wrong place).

## 8. Success went to my head, or I can't take feedback

**Ask first**
- When did you last change your mind because of someone's feedback?
- How much of your last win was luck or timing?
- Who around you tells you the truth?

**Plan**
1. Name your phase and its ego risk (Aspire, Succeed, Fail, M11).
2. Review the last big win for luck and mistakes (Failure Inside the Success, M11, M07).
3. Book time with customers and front-line staff; be a student again (M11).
4. Notice fixed-mindset reactions to criticism (M09).
5. Ask two honest people monthly where ego is showing (M11, M04).

**Sign to watch:** decisions changed because of feedback per month.
**Common mistake:** swinging from ego to self-loathing. The aim is earned confidence.

## 9. Nothing is ever enough

**Ask first**
- What number did you once think would be enough? What happened when you hit it?
- Who are you comparing yourself to?
- What would you do with your time if you were already there?

**Plan**
1. Write the Enough Number: income, hours, size (M07).
2. Check the current plan against it: does each move get you there or move the target (M07)?
3. Stop spending to impress (M07, M13).
4. Replace status measures with values in your control (Values Audit, M13).
5. Practice wanting less: list desires you can drop (M08).

**Sign to watch:** moves turned down because they fail the enough test.
**Common mistake:** defining enough then raising it at the next milestone. Review it yearly, not on good months.

## 10. The business failed or I have to close it

**Ask first**
- What part of this can still be changed or salvaged?
- What are you telling yourself this says about you?
- Who is depending on you now, and what do they need?

**Plan**
1. Fix or salvage what can be changed first; take the closure mechanics to Business (M03).
2. Separate the event from the identity story (Story Check, M02; Fail phase, M11).
3. Use the time: alive time, not dead time (M11).
4. Choose your stance and find one deed the loss can serve (M03).
5. List what you learned and what was luck (M11, M07).

**Sign to watch:** a next project or role chosen and started.
**Common mistake:** deciding you are a failure rather than that a venture failed.
**Hand-off:** grief, shame or low mood that persists goes to Psychology and Mental Health.

## 11. I know exactly what to do and I keep not doing it

**Ask first**
- What is the behavior, and how often does it repeat?
- What have you tried (habits, tools, pressure)?
- What might you lose if you succeeded?

**Plan**
1. Check the system first; if no habit or calendar exists, hand off to Productivity.
2. Map the self-sabotage: what need does not doing it protect (M14)?
3. Name the belief and write a believable rung (M14).
4. Meet the need another way and take one small step (M14).
5. If it is plain avoidance with no deeper need, use the Accountability Mirror for a month (M01).

**Sign to watch:** days per week the behavior happens.
**Common mistake:** adding more willpower or apps to a conflict between needs.

## 12. I can't stop worrying about things I can't control

**Ask first**
- What are the worries, specifically?
- Which have you acted on, and which do you just replay?
- Is the worry constant, or tied to specific events?

**Plan**
1. Run the Control Sort on each worry (M02, M10).
2. For each controllable item set one action; for influence items one ask (M10).
3. For the rest, write a short response plan if it happens, then stop rehearsing (Premeditation of Adversity, M10, M02).
4. Use Let Them for people's choices (M05).
5. Each morning, expect some setbacks and decide your stance in advance (M02).

**Sign to watch:** time per day spent replaying uncontrollable items.
**Common mistake:** treating a constant, physical anxiety as a mindset problem. That goes to Psychology and Mental Health.

## 13. I need a hard push for a launch, a deadline or a sales drought

**Ask first**
- Is the plan sound, and has anyone checked it?
- How are your sleep, health and family load right now?
- How long does the push need to be?

**Plan**
1. Confirm the plan with Business; grit on a wrong plan wastes effort.
2. Set a Defined Push: one target, start and end dates, the daily hard action (M01).
3. Set guardrails: sleep, a rest day, family time (M01 limits, M12).
4. Use the Accountability Mirror and Cookie Jar for the wobbles (M01).
5. Review at the end and return to a normal pace.

**Sign to watch:** daily hard action done, and sleep hours.
**Common mistake:** a push with no end date that becomes a way of life.

## 14. "I'm just not a numbers person" (or a sales person, or a leader)

**Ask first**
- Where did you learn that about yourself?
- What would you need to change to succeed at the next level?
- Who already does this well and could teach you?

**Plan**
1. Rewrite the trait as not yet with a strategy (M09).
2. Log the triggers that bring the fixed voice up (M09).
3. Pick one weak spot and build a Deliberate Practice Loop (M12).
4. Praise your own process, not results, in weekly reviews (M09).
5. Decide whether the skill is worth growing or better delegated (M09, M13).

**Sign to watch:** practice sessions per week and one measurable skill marker.
**Common mistake:** effort without strategy or feedback; that is not a growth mindset.

## 15. My family or friends doubt me, and it gets in my head

**Ask first**
- Whose opinion, exactly, and what do they say?
- Is any of their concern useful information?
- Are you seeking their approval or their support?

**Plan**
1. Sort useful concern from opinion (Story Check, M02).
2. Let them hold their view; decide what you will do (Let Them, Let Me, M05).
3. Find a few peers who see the work clearly (Mastermind, M04).
4. Choose values in your control as your measure (M13).
5. State any boundary once if comments keep crossing it (M05, M14).

**Sign to watch:** decisions delayed because of others' approval.
**Common mistake:** ignoring all feedback, including the useful kind.
**Hand-off:** the relationship itself goes to Relationships and Leadership.

## 16. I'm drained and want to push harder to get through it

**Ask first**
- How long have you felt this way?
- Are you sleeping, eating, and do you feel numb, hopeless or constantly anxious?
- When did you last take a full day off?

**Plan**
1. Do not prescribe Can't Hurt Me or The War of Art here. Pushing harder is the wrong answer.
2. Hand off to Psychology and Mental Health for burnout, sleep, anxiety or low mood.
3. From the Mindset side only: check whether the goalposts keep moving (Enough Number, M07) and whether the work still has meaning (M03).
4. Remove what can be removed before reframing anything (M03).

**Sign to watch:** whether the owner agrees to rest or get support this week.
**Common mistake:** treating exhaustion as weakness of will.
