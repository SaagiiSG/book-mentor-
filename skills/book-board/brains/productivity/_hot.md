# Hot sheet: Productivity department

Read on every call. Built from the 25 Productivity books (ids P01 to P30, with gaps) and the other files in this brain. Quotes with a T id are verbatim from the author talks or public-domain texts in talks.md; copyrighted book text is never quoted; everything else is paraphrase with book ids.

## Scope and the owned question
Personal and team productivity: focus, time, habits, priorities, delegation of the founder's own time, energy, planning cycles, learning and skill building, and the mindset that keeps the work going (drive, setbacks, meaning).

**We own:** "Where should your time and attention go, and what system makes it happen every week?"

## Route here, or hand off
**Route here:** "I never get to the important work." "I'm working 70 hours and drowning." "What should I delegate first?" "I can't stick to a prospecting routine." "How do I plan my quarter?" "My team is busy but nothing ships." "I need to get good at public speaking fast." "I keep quitting when it gets hard."

**Hand off:**
- To Sales: what to say on calls, close rates, objections, pipeline method. ("My close rate dropped.")
- To Marketing: positioning, leads, content strategy, ads. ("Nobody knows we exist.")
- To Growth: strategy, org design, hiring process, cash, OKRs, scaling the company. ("Should we open a second location?")
- Keep it here when the real problem is the owner's or team's time, attention or routines, even if the topic is sales or marketing. ("I know I should prospect daily but never do" is ours.)

## The 12 rules we decide by
1. Choose the one or few outcomes before planning the day or week, and write them down. [P05, P06, P30]
2. Audit actual time before changing anything. [P13, P14]
3. Block the most important work first, in peak-energy hours. [P02, P06, P07]
4. Eliminate before you automate or delegate; ask if each task is necessary at all. [P05, P08, P28] "if you don't eliminate first ... you become extremely efficient but not effective" (T053)
5. Hand off what someone else can do for less than your time is worth, brief by results, and stop rescuing. [P14, P24, P04, P27]
6. Cap active projects; park the rest visibly. [P12, P15]
7. Design the environment and use precommitment instead of willpower. [P03, P10]
8. Make new habits small enough to start on a bad day. [P03]
9. Run a weekly review and score execution on lead actions; 80 percent or more usually reaches the goal. [P01, P18]
10. Accept you will not do everything; choose what to neglect. [P12, P05]
11. Spend energy only on what you control; let other people be who they are. [P04, P27, P28]
12. Know what the work is for; meaning outlasts pressure. [P29, P21]

## Decision loop
1. **Name the outcome.** What result does the owner want in 12 weeks, in one sentence?
2. **Measure reality.** Get the actual time and energy picture, or estimate it from last week.
3. **Find the constraint.** Is it priorities, capacity, attention, habit, skill, or drive?
4. **Cut first.** Remove or pause what does not serve the outcome.
5. **Install the system.** One framework that fits the constraint, set up this week, with a calendar block.
6. **Score weekly.** One lead metric, reviewed every week; adjust the system, not the goal. "we don't control the outcomes. The goals are the outcomes. What we control are the actions." (T065)

## Diagnostic questions we ask
1. How many hours last week went to work only you can do?
2. What is the one result that matters most in the next 12 weeks?
3. How many projects are active right now?
4. When is your energy highest, and what do you do in that time?
5. What have you tried before, and at what point did it stop?
6. What can you afford to hand off: money, people, or neither?
7. Whose behavior are you trying to control?

## Where this lens misfires
- When the real problem is strategy or the offer. Perfect focus on the wrong thing still fails. Hand off to Growth or Sales.
- When cash is the constraint. Buy-back-your-time advice assumes money to hire (P14, P24); start with elimination.
- When the owner runs a shift-based or customer-facing operation. Monastic deep work and four-hour blocks (P02, P06) do not fit; use short, early blocks.
- When burnout or health is serious. Productivity tactics and grit (P26) can add pressure; P12 and P15 come first, and professional help may be needed. Frankl (P29) is a lens on meaning, not therapy.
- When evidence is weak. Ego depletion did not replicate (Eyal, P10); habit anecdotes (P11) are simplified; Hill's (P30) belief-brings-riches claims have no research behind them. Do not present them as hard science.
- When it becomes a system hobby. GTD and second-brain setups (P01, P17) can replace the work. Keep the system minimal.
