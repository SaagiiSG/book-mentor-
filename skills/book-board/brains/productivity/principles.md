# Principles: Productivity department

Built from the 20 Productivity books (P01 to P20). Decision rules with supporting ids; confidence counts how many of our books clearly support each.

Quote policy: verbatim quotes appear only from the author talks and public-domain texts listed in talks.md, always with a T id. Copyrighted book text is never quoted. Everything else is paraphrase cited by book id.

## Decision rules

### Priorities
1. Decide the one or few outcomes that matter before you plan the day or week. [P04, P05, P06, P07] Confidence: high, four books agree.
2. Measure a task by the consequence of doing or not doing it, not by how urgent it feels. [P04, P07, P12] Confidence: high, three books agree.
3. If a new commitment is not a clear yes, treat it as a no. [P05, P06, P14] Confidence: medium, three books agree; early-stage owners may need exceptions.
4. Decide what you will neglect on purpose, and write it down. [P05, P11, P12] Confidence: medium to high, three books agree.
5. Cap the number of active projects and keep the rest in a visible queue. [P11, P14, P05] Confidence: medium, three books agree.
6. Do one important thing at a time; switching costs quality. [P02, P06, P12] Confidence: high, three books agree.
7. Spend energy on what you control (your own choices and responses), not on other people or events. [P04] Confidence: medium, one book (Circle of Influence). For the mindset side: Mindset department (M05 The Let Them Theory).
8. Quality of output beats visible busyness as the measure of productive work. [P14, P02, P12] Confidence: medium, three books agree.
9. Before each task, ask whether it is necessary at all. [P05, P08, P12] Confidence: medium to high, three books agree.
10. Write a definite target with a deadline, and review it often. [P07, P16, P04] Confidence: medium to high, three books agree.

### Time and attention
11. Audit where your time actually goes before you change anything. [P12, P13, P08] Confidence: high, three books agree.
12. Put your most important work on the calendar first, as a protected block. [P02, P06, P09, P16, P12] Confidence: high, five books agree.
13. Do the most demanding task first, in your best hours. [P07, P06] Confidence: medium, two books agree.
14. Batch shallow work (email, messages, admin) into fixed slots. [P02, P08] Confidence: medium to high, two books agree.
15. Deal with the feeling behind distraction first, then remove external triggers, and add pacts last. [P09, P11] Confidence: medium, two books agree.
16. Give work a tight deadline, because it expands to fill the time you allow. [P08, P16] Confidence: medium, two books agree.
17. Keep some unstructured time for thinking and recovery; it is part of the work. [P02, P11, P16, P14] Confidence: medium to high, four books agree.

### Delegation of the owner's time
18. Hand off work that someone else can do at an acceptable standard for less than your time is worth. [P13, P20, P08, P12] Confidence: high, four books agree.
19. Eliminate before you automate or delegate. [P08, P05, P12] Confidence: high, three books agree.
20. Delegate results with clear success criteria, not step-by-step tasks. [P04, P20, P13] Confidence: high, three books agree.
21. Once delegated, let people own the result and their mistakes; stop taking the work back. [P04, P13] Confidence: medium, two books agree. Leading and coaching the person: Relationships and Leadership department.
22. Document a task once (record it) before handing it off. [P13, P08] Confidence: medium, two books agree.
23. Ask who could do it before asking how you would learn it; treat chronic procrastination on a task as a sign it is not yours. [P20, P13] Confidence: medium, two books agree; core skills still need learning.
24. Build on people's strengths when you assign work. [P12, P20] Confidence: medium, two books agree.

### Habits and systems
25. Build systems and habits; goals alone do not change behavior. [P03, P10, P18, P16] Confidence: high, four books agree. "we don't rise to the level of our goals, we fall to the level of our systems" (T047)
26. Make a new habit small enough to start on a bad day, then grow it. [P03] Confidence: medium, one book, and Clear calls it his most important rule.
27. Design the environment so the right action is the easy default. [P03, P09, P05] Confidence: high, three books agree.
28. Track the behavior you control, weekly, and judge yourself by execution. [P16, P18, P03] Confidence: high, three books agree.
29. To change a habit, keep the trigger and reward and swap the routine. [P10, P03] Confidence: medium, two books agree.
30. Look for a keystone habit that pulls other improvements along. [P10] Confidence: low to medium, one book.
31. Expect results to lag effort, and keep a sound routine running for at least a full cycle before judging it. [P03, P18, P16] Confidence: medium, three books agree.
32. Use design and precommitment instead of willpower in the moment. [P09, P03] Confidence: high on the practice. Eyal says ego depletion failed to replicate, so do not treat willpower as a fuel tank.
33. Get a peer or small group to see your weekly score. [P16, P10] Confidence: medium, two books agree.

### Planning cycles
34. Run a weekly review at a fixed time; it keeps every other system alive. [P01, P04, P16] Confidence: high, three books agree.
35. Plan in short cycles (about 12 weeks) with weekly scoring, rather than only yearly goals. [P16, P06] Confidence: medium, two books support short cascades.
36. Get every open commitment out of your head into one trusted place. [P01, P15] Confidence: medium to high, two books agree.
37. Give every project a concrete next action. [P01, P15] Confidence: medium, two books agree.

### Learning and skill
38. Improve a skill with targeted practice, feedback and stretch, not just more hours. [P19, P17] Confidence: medium, two books agree.
39. Match challenge to skill to keep yourself and your team engaged. [P17, P19] Confidence: medium, two books agree.

### Limits and energy
40. Accept that you will never finish everything; productivity is choosing well, not clearing the list. [P11, P05, P14] Confidence: medium to high, three books agree.
41. Protect sleep, health and rest as the base of output. [P05, P06, P04] Confidence: high, three books agree. Sleep problems, anxiety or burnout as health issues: Psychology and Mental Health department.
42. Judge the week's activity against a written mission, and say no to what does not serve it. [P04, P05] Confidence: medium, two books agree. For meaning itself: Mindset department (M03 Man's Search for Meaning).
43. When a setback hits, separate what you can still act on from what you cannot, and put the next action on the calendar. Confidence: medium, synthesis of [P04, P01]. For the setback itself: Mindset department (M02 Meditations).

## Where the books disagree

### 1. Ferriss versus Covey on delegation and presence
P08 says remove yourself and outsource aggressively, with rules so helpers act without you. P04 says delegate by stewardship and invest in relationships and character over time.
**Ferriss is right when** tasks are repetitive, rule-based, and low in relationship value.
**Covey is right when** the work involves judgment, clients or culture, and the owner's absence would weaken trust.

### 2. Big daily focus block versus short or tiny starts
P06 argues for about four hours a day; P16 uses about three-hour blocks; P02's rhythmic block fits any length; P03 starts a habit at two minutes.
**Long blocks are right when** the owner controls their calendar and the work is creative or strategic.
**Short blocks are right when** the owner runs a shop floor, sells all day, or is rebuilding the habit from zero.

### 3. Systems that capture everything versus systems that limit intake
P01 and P15 say capture every input and organize it. P11, P14 and P08 say cut inputs and accept that much will never be done.
**Capture is right when** dropped balls are hurting customers or the team.
**Limiting is right when** the system has become a growing list that causes guilt and no output.

### 4. Make it easy versus plan for the hard
P03 says make habits easy and satisfying. P09 says effortful work is never a true habit, so schedule it and use pacts.
**Easy is right when** a routine keeps failing to start.
**Planning and pacts are right when** the work is hard by nature (a proposal, a difficult call) and will never feel automatic. If the owner has the plan and the pact and still quits at the first discomfort: Mindset department (M01 Can't Hurt Me).

### 5. Hustle and urgency versus slow and sustainable pace
P16 and P07 push urgency, effort and doing the hard task now. P14 and P11 argue for fewer things at a natural pace.
**Urgency is right when** a plan exists but execution is drifting, or a cash deadline is real.
**Slow is right when** the owner is overcommitted, quality is slipping, or burnout signs show (and for burnout itself, hand off to Psychology and Mental Health).

### 6. Who Not How versus learning it yourself (Peak)
P20 says find a Who; P19 says skill comes from deliberate practice you do yourself.
**Who is right when** the skill is peripheral or rare (legal, design).
**Practice is right when** the skill is core to the owner's role (selling the offer, leading people, reading the numbers).

### 7. Hiring to buy time versus eliminating first
P13 treats hiring as the main lever. P05 and P08 put elimination first, and P12 asks what happens if a task is not done at all.
**Hire when** the task is necessary, frequent, and below your buyback rate, and cash allows.
**Eliminate first when** cash is tight or the task's value is unclear.

### 8. Optimize productivity versus question productivity itself
P01, P06, P07 and P16 aim to get more done. P11 says efficiency invites more demands, so the list never ends.
**Optimize when** the owner's choices are sound but execution is weak.
**Question it when** the owner is already efficient but exhausted. If the real gap is why the work matters: Mindset department (M03 Man's Search for Meaning).

### 9. Habits as identity versus habits as loops
P03 centers identity ("become the type of person"). P10 centers the cue, routine and reward loop.
**Identity framing helps when** the owner keeps quitting because the habit feels foreign.
**Loop analysis helps when** a specific bad routine has a clear trigger to diagnose.
