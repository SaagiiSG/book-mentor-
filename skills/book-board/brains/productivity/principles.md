# Principles: Productivity department

Built from the 25 Productivity books (P01 to P25). Decision rules in plain sentences, each with supporting ids and a confidence note. Confidence counts how many of our books clearly support the rule.

## Decision rules

### Priorities
1. Decide the one or few outcomes that matter before you plan the day or week. [P04, P05, P06, P07, P09, P25] Confidence: high, six books agree.
2. Measure a task by the consequence of doing or not doing it, not by how urgent it feels. [P04, P07, P13] Confidence: high, three books agree.
3. If a new commitment is not a clear yes, treat it as a no. [P05, P06, P15] Confidence: medium, three books agree, and early-stage owners may need exceptions.
4. Decide what you will neglect on purpose, and write it down. [P05, P12, P13] Confidence: medium to high, three books agree.
5. Cap the number of active projects and keep the rest in a visible queue. [P12, P15, P05] Confidence: medium, three books agree.
6. Do one important thing at a time; switching costs quality. [P02, P06, P13, P16] Confidence: high, four books agree.
7. Spend energy on what you can influence, not on what you only worry about. [P04] Confidence: low to medium, one book, but uncontested.
8. Quality of output beats visible busyness as the measure of productive work. [P15, P02, P13] Confidence: medium, three books agree.

### Time and attention
9. Audit where your time actually goes before you change anything. [P13, P14, P25] Confidence: high, three books agree.
10. Put your most important work on the calendar first, as a protected block. [P02, P06, P10, P18, P13] Confidence: high, five books agree.
11. Place the most demanding work in your highest-energy hours. [P25, P09, P07] Confidence: medium, three books agree.
12. Batch shallow work (email, messages, admin) into fixed slots. [P02, P08, P25] Confidence: high, three books agree.
13. Turn off notifications and remove triggers before you rely on discipline. [P10, P09, P03, P02] Confidence: high, four books agree.
14. Treat distraction as an escape from discomfort and deal with the feeling first. [P10, P25] Confidence: medium, two books agree.
15. Give work a tight deadline, because it expands to fill the time you allow. [P08, P18] Confidence: medium, two books agree.
16. Keep some unstructured time for thinking and recovery; it is part of the work. [P16, P12, P18, P15] Confidence: medium, four books agree.

### Delegation of the owner's time
17. Hand off work that someone else can do at an acceptable standard for less than your time is worth. [P14, P24, P08, P13] Confidence: high, four books agree.
18. Eliminate before you automate or delegate. [P08, P05, P13] Confidence: high, three books agree.
19. Delegate results with clear success criteria, not step-by-step tasks. [P04, P24, P14] Confidence: high, three books agree.
20. Document a task once (record it) before handing it off. [P14, P08] Confidence: medium, two books agree.
21. Ask who could do it before asking how you would learn it. [P24, P14] Confidence: medium, two books agree, with the limit that core skills still need learning.
22. Build on people's strengths when you assign work. [P13, P24] Confidence: medium, two books agree.

### Habits and systems
23. Build systems and habits; goals alone do not change behavior. [P03, P11, P22, P18] Confidence: high, four books agree.
24. Make a new habit small enough to start on a bad day. [P03, P19] Confidence: medium to high, two books agree strongly.
25. Design the environment so the right action is the easy default. [P03, P10, P20, P05] Confidence: high, four books agree.
26. Track the behavior you control, weekly, and judge yourself by execution. [P18, P22, P20, P03] Confidence: high, four books agree.
27. To change a habit, keep the trigger and reward and swap the routine. [P11, P03] Confidence: medium, two books agree.
28. Look for a keystone habit that pulls other improvements along. [P11] Confidence: low to medium, one book.
29. Expect results to lag effort, and keep a sound routine running for at least a full cycle before judging it. [P03, P22, P18] Confidence: medium, three books agree.
30. Use precommitment and routines instead of relying on willpower in the moment. [P20, P10, P03] Confidence: high on the practice, low on the willpower-as-fuel theory, which has replication problems.

### Planning cycles
31. Run a weekly review at a fixed time; it keeps every other system alive. [P01, P04, P18, P09] Confidence: high, four books agree.
32. Plan in short cycles (about 12 weeks) with weekly scoring, rather than only yearly goals. [P18, P06] Confidence: medium, two books support short cascades.
33. Get every open commitment out of your head into one trusted place. [P01, P17, P20] Confidence: high, three books agree.
34. Give every project a concrete next action. [P01, P17] Confidence: medium, two books agree.

### Learning and skill
35. Improve a skill with targeted practice, feedback and stretch, not just more hours. [P23, P21] Confidence: medium, two books agree.
36. Match challenge to skill to keep yourself and your team engaged. [P21, P23] Confidence: medium, two books agree.

### Limits
37. Accept that you will never finish everything; productivity is choosing well, not clearing the list. [P12, P05, P15] Confidence: medium to high, three books agree.
38. Protect sleep, health and rest as the base of output. [P05, P06, P09, P25, P04] Confidence: high, five books agree.

## Where the books disagree

### 1. Ferriss versus Covey on delegation and presence
P08 says remove yourself and outsource aggressively, with rules so helpers act without you. P04 says delegate by stewardship and invest in relationships and character over time.
**Ferriss is right when** tasks are repetitive, rule-based, and low in relationship value (inbox triage, scheduling, order processing).
**Covey is right when** the work involves judgment, clients or culture, and the owner's absence would weaken trust.

### 2. Big daily focus block versus short or flexible focus
P06 argues for about four hours a day on the one thing; P18 uses about three-hour strategic blocks; P09 asks for one daily Highlight of 60 to 90 minutes (detail uncertain on exact length); P19 starts tiny.
**Long blocks are right when** the owner controls their calendar and the work is creative or strategic.
**Short blocks are right when** the owner runs a shop floor, sells all day, or is rebuilding the habit from zero.

### 3. Systems that capture everything versus systems that limit intake
P01 and P17 say capture every input and organize it. P12, P15 and P08 say cut inputs and accept that much will never be done.
**Capture is right when** dropped balls are hurting customers or the team.
**Limiting is right when** the system has become a growing list that causes guilt and no output.

### 4. Willpower as a resource versus willpower as a mindset or habit
P20 treats self-control as a depletable resource. P19 relies on small daily willpower. P10 and P03 shift the work to environment and pacts. The resource model has replication problems.
**Use routines and precommitment** in all cases. Treat "my willpower is drained" as a signal to change design, not as a fixed law.

### 5. Hustle and urgency versus slow and sustainable pace
P18 and P07 push urgency and doing the hard task now. P15 and P12 argue for fewer things at a natural pace.
**Urgency is right when** a plan exists but execution is drifting, or a cash deadline is real.
**Slow is right when** the owner is overcommitted, quality is slipping, or burnout signs show.

### 6. Who Not How versus learning it yourself (Peak)
P24 says find a Who; P23 says skill comes from deliberate practice you do yourself.
**Who is right when** the skill is peripheral or rare (legal, design, ad buying).
**Practice is right when** the skill is core to the owner's role (selling the offer, leading people, reading the numbers).

### 7. Hiring to buy time versus eliminating first
P14 treats hiring as the main lever. P05 and P08 put elimination first, and P13 asks what happens if a task is not done at all.
**Hire when** the task is necessary, frequent, and below your buyback rate, and cash allows.
**Eliminate first when** cash is tight or the task's value is unclear.

### 8. Optimize productivity versus question productivity itself
Most of the list (P01, P06, P07, P18, P25) aims to get more done. P12 argues that efficiency invites more demands and the goal should be meaning, not volume.
**Optimize when** the owner's choices are sound but execution is weak.
**Question it when** the owner is already efficient but exhausted and unsure why it matters.

### 9. Habits as identity versus habits as loops
P03 centers identity ("become the type of person"). P11 centers the cue, routine and reward loop.
**Identity framing helps when** the owner keeps quitting because the habit feels foreign.
**Loop analysis helps when** a specific bad routine has a clear trigger to diagnose.
