# Playbooks: Productivity department

Built from the 25 Productivity books (P01 to P25) and the frameworks in frameworks.md. Each playbook is a situation owners bring, with questions to ask first, a plan, a metric, and the common mistake.

## 1. I can't find time to work on the business

**Ask first**
- How many hours last week went to work only you can do, versus work someone else could do?
- What is the one project that would change the business most this quarter?
- When in the day is your energy highest, and what are you doing then?
- What happens if a task you do today is simply not done?

**Plan**
1. Log time for one week in 30-minute slots (Know Thy Time, P13; audit, P14).
2. Tag each block on the DRIP Matrix (P14) and mark what could be dropped (P13, P05).
3. Choose one "work on" project with the Focusing Question (P06).
4. Block two to three protected sessions a week in peak hours for it (Time Blocking, P02, P06, P18; Biological Prime Time, P25).
5. Batch email and admin into two slots a day (P08, P02).
6. Hand off or drop the top two draining, low-value tasks (Buyback Rate, P14; Explore, Eliminate, Execute, P05).
7. Review every Friday (Weekly Review, P01, P18).

**Metric:** hours per week spent in protected "work on the business" blocks.
**Common mistake:** blocking the time but letting customers and staff book over it. The block must be treated like a client meeting.

## 2. I'm overwhelmed and dropping balls

**Ask first**
- Where do your commitments live right now (head, email, notes, sticky notes)?
- How many active projects do you have?
- When did you last review everything you owe people?

**Plan**
1. Do a full mind sweep onto one list (Capture, P01).
2. Clarify each item: actionable or not, and the next action (P01).
3. Do every item under two minutes immediately (P01).
4. Sort into projects, next actions, waiting-for, calendar, someday (P01; PARA for reference material, P17).
5. Cap active projects and park the rest visibly (Fixed-Volume Lists, P12; pull-based work, P15).
6. Tell people what is parked and when you will return to it (posteriorities, P13).
7. Start a weekly review at a fixed time (P01).

**Metric:** number of dropped commitments (missed follow-ups, late deliveries) per week.
**Common mistake:** building an elaborate app setup instead of clearing the backlog. Paper and one list are enough to start.

## 3. I'm the bottleneck: everything goes through me

**Ask first**
- What decisions or approvals wait on you each week?
- Which of these could follow a written rule?
- Who on the team could own a result if it were defined?
- What would break if you were away for two weeks?

**Plan**
1. List every recurring decision and task that waits on you (P13, P14).
2. Turn repeat decisions into rules or policies (Effective Decisions: generic problems get policies, P13; rules for helpers, P08).
3. Record each repetitive task once and write a playbook from it (Camcorder Method, P14).
4. Delegate results with the five stewardship parts (P04) or an Impact Filter (P24).
5. Set a spending or decision limit staff can act within without asking (P08).
6. Hold a short weekly check-in instead of ad hoc approvals (P18 WAM style).
7. Run a test: one week away from day-to-day approvals (DEAL Liberation, P08).

**Metric:** number of items per week waiting on owner approval.
**Common mistake:** delegating the task but keeping the decision, so work still stops at the owner.
**Hand off:** if the issue is org structure or roles, hand off to Growth.

## 4. I don't know what to hire or delegate first

**Ask first**
- What is your rough hourly value, and could you pay someone a fraction of it?
- Which tasks drain you, and which do you love?
- What cash can you commit monthly to help?

**Plan**
1. Audit two weeks of time (P13, P14).
2. Place tasks on the DRIP Matrix (P14).
3. Compute a buyback threshold and list tasks below it (Buyback Rate, P14).
4. Eliminate first anything with no real consequence (P05, P08, P13).
5. Start with admin and inbox help, usually the first rung of the replacement order (Replacement Ladder, P14).
6. Brief the hire with an Impact Filter and recorded playbooks (P24, P14).
7. Fill the freed hours with production or investment work, and check that you did (Buyback Loop: Fill, P14).

**Metric:** owner hours per week freed and reinvested in top-value work.
**Common mistake:** hiring help and then filling the freed time with more low-value work.
**Hand off:** hiring process and role design go to Growth (G24, Who).

## 5. I set goals but never follow through

**Ask first**
- What were last quarter's goals and what happened by week six?
- Did each goal have weekly actions written down?
- Who sees your progress besides you?

**Plan**
1. Cut to one to three goals for the next 12 weeks (12 Week Year, P18; Focusing Question, P06).
2. Write weekly tactics for each goal (P18).
3. Block performance time for them (P18, P06).
4. Score execution every week, aiming for about 85 percent (Weekly Scorecard, P18).
5. Track lead indicators, not only results (P18, P22).
6. Join or form a weekly accountability check-in (WAM, P18).
7. Expect the dip after week three and plan for it (P18; Plateau of Latent Potential, P03).

**Metric:** weekly execution score (share of planned tactics completed).
**Common mistake:** setting ten goals. Execution collapses when focus spreads.

## 6. I'm distracted all day

**Ask first**
- What do you reach for when you get distracted, and what were you feeling just before?
- Which notifications are on right now?
- Did you plan your day in the calendar, or only in a list?

**Plan**
1. Define traction for tomorrow by timeboxing the calendar (Indistractable, P10).
2. Turn off all non-essential notifications and move apps off the home screen (Laser, P09; external triggers, P10).
3. When the urge hits, name the feeling and wait ten minutes (internal triggers, P10).
4. Set email and message check times (Batching, P08, P02).
5. Add one effort pact, such as a site blocker during focus blocks (P10; environment design, P03).
6. Single-task each block with only one window open (P02, P16).

**Metric:** number of focus blocks per week completed without switching.
**Common mistake:** blaming the phone and ignoring the uncomfortable task underneath.

## 7. I procrastinate on the important hard task

**Ask first**
- What exactly is the task, and what is the very first physical step?
- Which feels true: boring, frustrating, difficult, ambiguous, unstructured, or meaningless?
- What happens if it is not done in a month?

**Plan**
1. Name the consequence of not doing it (P07, P04).
2. Score the procrastination triggers and reverse each one (P25).
3. Define the next physical action (P01).
4. Shrink the first session to 15 to 30 minutes (Mini Habits, P19; two-minute rule, P03).
5. Do it first thing tomorrow (Eat That Frog, P07; Highlight, P09).
6. Use fear-setting if the task is a scary decision (P08).

**Metric:** days the task has been open; target it moving within 48 hours.
**Common mistake:** waiting to feel ready. Start small and let action create motivation.

## 8. I'm burning out on 60-plus hour weeks

**Ask first**
- How many hours did you work last week, and how much sleep did you get?
- Which hours produced real results?
- What are you afraid will happen if you work less?
- What work could stop with no real harm?

**Plan**
1. Audit hours and energy for one week (P13, P25).
2. Set a hard stop time and a shutdown ritual (P02; Parkinson's law, P08).
3. Decide what to neglect this quarter and tell people (P12, P13, P05).
4. Cap active projects (P15, P12).
5. Protect sleep and exercise as non-negotiable blocks (P05, P09, P06).
6. Hand off the most draining low-value tasks (P14).
7. Plan a lighter season or breakaway time (P15, P18).

**Metric:** weekly hours worked, with output (revenue or key deliverables) held steady.
**Common mistake:** treating burnout as a time-management problem only. Often the fix is fewer commitments, not faster work (P12, P15).
**Note:** if there are signs of depression or health issues, recommend professional help; this department does not replace it.

## 9. My team has too many projects and nothing finishes

**Ask first**
- How many active projects are there per person?
- Who decides what starts?
- Is there a visible list of what is in progress and what is waiting?

**Plan**
1. List every active initiative and owner (P15).
2. Apply extreme criteria to each and cut or pause (P05).
3. Set a work-in-progress cap per person and team (P15, P12).
4. Build a visible holding queue that new work enters (pull-based workload, P15).
5. Agree each remaining project's success criteria (Impact Filter, P24).
6. Review the board weekly and pull new work only when something ships (P18, P01).

**Metric:** projects completed per month and average days per project.
**Common mistake:** pausing projects but letting them keep generating meetings.
**Hand off:** for company strategy or OKRs, hand off to Growth (G12, G03).

## 10. I can't stick to a routine

**Ask first**
- What routine, exactly, and how often have you tried?
- What is the trigger meant to be, and where are you when it should happen?
- What is the smallest version you could do on your worst day?

**Plan**
1. Shrink the routine to a mini version (P19, P03).
2. Anchor it to an existing habit with a time and place (habit stacking, P03).
3. Remove friction and add cues in the environment (P03, P10).
4. Pair it with a reward or something enjoyable (P03, P11).
5. Track it daily and never miss twice (P03, P22).
6. Grow it only once it is automatic (P19).

**Metric:** days per week the routine is done.
**Common mistake:** restarting at full size after a lapse instead of at the mini version.

## 11. Meetings and messages eat the week

**Ask first**
- How many hours a week are in meetings, and which produce decisions?
- What is the expected reply time on chat and email?
- Who can book your calendar?

**Plan**
1. Measure shallow work hours (Drain the Shallows, P02).
2. Cancel or shorten meetings with no decision or owner (P13 on too many meetings; P05).
3. Set team response-time norms and check times (P08, P10).
4. Replace status meetings with a weekly written update (P15, P02).
5. Create meeting-free blocks for the team (Time Blocking, P02, P06).
6. Sync your schedule with your team or manager (schedule sync, P10).

**Metric:** meeting hours per week, and share of week spent in deep work.
**Common mistake:** cutting meetings but letting chat become the meeting, all day.

## 12. I need to learn a new skill fast

**Ask first**
- Is this skill core to your role, or can a Who do it?
- What does good look like, and who can show you?
- How will you get feedback each week?

**Plan**
1. Decide learn versus hand off (Who Not How, P24; tension with Peak, P23).
2. Break the skill into sub-skills and pick the weakest (Deliberate Practice, P23).
3. Set a measurable practice target (P23).
4. Practice in short, focused sessions (P23, P02).
5. Get feedback through a coach, recordings, or data (P23).
6. Keep the challenge just above current skill (Flow, P21).
7. Save what you learn as reusable notes (CODE, P17).

**Metric:** a skill-specific output measure (for example, proposals accepted or calls to booked meetings).
**Common mistake:** consuming courses and books instead of practicing with feedback.
**Hand off:** if the skill is selling, hand off to Sales for the method itself.

## 13. How should I plan my week and quarter?

**Ask first**
- Do you have goals for the next 12 weeks?
- When do you plan, and how long does it take?
- Do you plan in a list or in the calendar?

**Plan**
1. Set one to three 12-week goals (P18, P06).
2. Each week, run a review: clear inboxes, update lists, score last week (P01, P18).
3. Choose the week's top priorities by role or goal (P04; Rule of 3, P25).
4. Block priorities first, then buffer time for admin (P04, P18, P06).
5. Each day, pick a Highlight or frog and do it first (P09, P07).
6. Close each day with a short shutdown (P02).

**Metric:** weekly execution score and planning done on time each week.
**Common mistake:** planning the week with no room for interruptions. Add buffer blocks (P05, P18).

## 14. I have no time to think strategically

**Ask first**
- When did you last spend an hour thinking with no screen?
- What big question are you avoiding?
- Where do your best ideas usually come from?

**Plan**
1. Schedule one weekly thinking block away from the desk (Scatterfocus, P16; bimodal depth, P02).
2. Keep a short list of open strategic questions (12 favorite problems idea, P17).
3. Capture ideas as they come into one place (P01, P17).
4. Use walks and simple tasks for deliberate mind wandering (P16).
5. Turn insights into one next action each (P01).
6. Hand the strategy question itself to Growth if it is about market or model.

**Metric:** thinking blocks held per month and decisions that came out of them.
**Common mistake:** filling the thinking block with email "just to clear it first".

## 15. Delegated work keeps coming back wrong

**Ask first**
- How did you brief it: steps, or the result and why?
- What standard did you define, and how would they know it was met?
- When did you check in, and how?

**Plan**
1. Rewrite the brief as results, guidelines, resources, accountability and consequences (Stewardship Delegation, P04).
2. Add success criteria with an Impact Filter (P24).
3. Record yourself doing it once and share it (Camcorder Method, P14).
4. Match the task to the person's strengths (P13).
5. Set a midpoint review, not constant check-ins (P04).
6. Fix the process, not the person, on the first miss (P14, P11 on routines).

**Metric:** share of delegated tasks accepted on first review.
**Common mistake:** taking the work back after one bad result, which teaches the team that the owner will always rescue it.
